# KINGMAKER — PUZZLE CONTENT
## KM_Game_Subsystems.md | Player-Solved Puzzles for Dungeons and Ruins

> **DM:** These puzzles require the PLAYER to solve them — not a skill check.
> When a puzzle triggers, present the description and WAIT.
> Do NOT offer a skill check bypass unless the player has been stuck for 3+ inputs
> without progress. One hint available per puzzle (player asks, DM gives it — no check).
> Solutions are in the DM SOLUTIONS section at the bottom — eyes only.

---

## ⚙️ PUZZLE RULES

**How to present:** Describe the puzzle fully. Show any inscriptions exactly as written.
Then stop. Say: *"What do you do?"* No choice menu. No skill check prompt.

**Hint rule:** Player may ask for ONE hint per puzzle. DM gives a genuine hint — not a
nudge toward a skill check, but an actual clue that narrows the solution space.
After a hint is given: `[1 hint used — no further hints available for this puzzle]`

**Skill check bypass:** Only available after 3+ player inputs that show genuine attempts
that are clearly off-track. Then offer: *"You could attempt a Recall Knowledge
([relevant skill] DC [X]) — success gives you the answer but no XP bonus."*
Solving it yourself: +50 XP bonus. Solving via skill check: no bonus.

**No puzzle has a combat solution.** If the player attacks the puzzle mechanism, it resets
or locks permanently (DM's call based on puzzle type).

## PUZZLE 1 — THE FOUR VIRTUES (Ch1, Ancient Tomb)

**Location:** The sealed inner chamber of the Ancient Tomb, Stolen Lands.
**Trigger:** Player enters the inner sanctum. Four stone pedestals, each with a shallow depression.

> *Four stone pedestals form a square around a central altar. Each pedestal bears a carved word in Old Taldane. On the altar: four smooth discs — gold, silver, iron, and obsidian.*
>
> *The pedestals read, clockwise from the north:*
> ***FORTUNA. VIRTUS. SAPIENTIA. CONCORDIA.***
>
> *Below each word, a line of smaller text. North: "What soldiers pray for before battle."*
> *East: "What soldiers are remembered for after death."*
> *South: "What generals possess that soldiers do not."*
> *West: "What holds an army together when all else fails."*
>
> *The discs sit on the altar. Each fits any depression exactly.*

**DM hint (if asked):** *"Consider what each word actually means in Taldane. Fortuna is fortune. Virtus is courage or excellence. Sapientia is wisdom. Concordia is harmony."*

## PUZZLE 2 — THE WARDEN'S LOCK (Ch1, Stag Lord's Fort)

**Location:** The armory vault door, basement level of the Stag Lord's fort.
**Trigger:** Player finds the vault door — heavy iron, three rotating dials with symbols.

> *The vault door has three iron dials. Each dial has eight symbols: a stag, a sword, a crown, a chain, a coin, a wolf, a skull, and a flame.*
>
> *Scratched into the wall beside the door, half-obscured by grime:*
> ***"The lord takes. The lord keeps. The lord burns what he cannot hold."***
>
> *Beneath that, in different handwriting, smaller:*
> ***"First his pride. Then his purpose. Last his mercy — which he has none of."***

**DM hint (if asked):** *"The scratched message describes three things about the Stag Lord, in order. Each dial corresponds to one."*

## PUZZLE 3 — THE MOON AND THE TIDE (Old Beldame's Test)

**Location:** Old Beldame's hut — she poses this before granting full shop access (if player didn't complete her quest).
**Trigger:** She stops the player at the threshold. "Answer me one thing first."

> **OLD BELDAME:** *"I have been here for forty years. In that time I have watched three things remain constant: the moon rises, the tide follows, and fools ask me for things without understanding what they're asking for.*
>
> *Here is my question. I have something that the more you take, the more you leave behind. What is it?"*

**DM hint (if asked):** Old Beldame raises an eyebrow. *"Think about the physical act of taking. What do you leave in the place of what you removed?"*

---

## PUZZLE 4 — THE CYCLOPS CALENDAR (Ch3, Vordakai's Tomb Entrance)

**Location:** The outer gate of Vordakai's Tomb. A circular stone disc blocks the door.
**Trigger:** Party reaches the sealed entrance. No amount of force opens it.

> *A circular stone disc fills the doorway entirely. Around its edge: 24 symbols arranged in a ring. In the center: a carved eye, closed.*
>
> *Above the door, in Ancient Cyclops script (Occultism DC 10 to read — this is a translation check, not a puzzle bypass):*
> ***"The eye opens when the dead hour arrives. Count from when the world was young."***
>
> *Three of the 24 symbols are worn differently than the others — more smoothed, as if touched repeatedly by many hands over many years. They are at positions 1, 13, and 24 in the ring.*

**DM hint (if asked):** *"Ancient Cyclops culture divided a day into 24 hours. The 'dead hour' in Cyclops mythology was the moment between the last hour of night and the first hour of dawn — the hour that belongs to neither day nor night. They called it the hour of the tomb."*

---

## PUZZLE 5 — THE FIVE MASKS (Ch3, Valley of the Dead)

**Location:** A shrine chamber in the Valley of the Dead.
**Trigger:** Five stone masks on the wall. A locked gate in the center.

> *Five stone masks hang on the wall in a row. Each depicts a different expression:*
> *Joy. Sorrow. Rage. Fear. Nothing.*
>
> *Below each mask, a hook. Below the hooks, this inscription:*
> ***"Wear what you are. The gate knows liars."***
>
> *In the corner, a pile of five actual masks — wooden, painted to match the stone ones. Each fits a human face.*
>
> *The gate has a single handprint impression beside it.*

**DM hint (if asked):** *"The inscription says 'wear what you are' — not 'wear what you want to be' or 'wear what is strongest.' The gate apparently knows if you're lying about it."*

---

## PUZZLE 6 — THE RIVER KINGS' RIDDLE (Ch4, Pitax Library)

**Location:** Hidden vault in Irovetti's private library — behind a false shelf.
**Trigger:** Player finds the vault door. A speaking stone beside it says:

> *"I am the beginning of everything, the end of everywhere, the beginning of eternity, and the end of time and space. What am I?"*

**DM hint (if asked):** *"Don't think about concepts. Think about the letters in those words."*

## PUZZLE 7 — THE GENERAL'S GAME (Ch4, Armag's Tomb, Trial of Strategy)

**Location:** A chamber in Armag's Tomb with a large stone table.
**Trigger:** A stone figure of a general stands at one end of a grid carved into the table. Four carved enemy pieces face it.

> *A grid of 5×5 squares is carved into the stone table. A general's piece — a tall carved warrior — stands in the center back row. Four enemy pieces are arranged facing it.*
>
> *On the wall: "The general does not run. The general does not die. The general reaches the far side."*
>
> *Each enemy piece has a movement carved on its base:*
> *Red piece: moves one square in any direction.*
> *Blue piece: moves two squares straight only.*
> *Green piece: moves diagonally only.*
> *Black piece: cannot move — it blocks.*
>
> *There are small stone markers on the table the player can use to track moves.*

**DM hint (if asked):** *"The black piece doesn't move — that's not a threat, that's a tool. And the blue piece can only go straight, which means it can be baited."*

## PUZZLE 8 — THE NYRISSA CIPHER (Ch6, Thousandbreaths)

**Location:** A chamber in Thousandbreaths with text covering every wall.
**Trigger:** Player notices that certain words are slightly raised compared to the others.

> *The walls of this circular chamber are covered in writing — dense, overlapping text in dozens of languages and scripts. It's overwhelming.*
>
> *On close inspection (Perception DC 10 — this is observation, not a bypass):*
> *Certain words throughout the text are raised slightly, as if embossed rather than carved. They catch the light differently.*
>
> *You count them. There are exactly nine.*
>
> *Written in order of discovery, they are:*
> ***HEART. GIVEN. STOLEN. LOVE. CURSED. REMEMBER. FORGET. KING. LAUGH.***

**DM hint (if asked):** *"What story do you already know? What happened to Nyrissa that you've learned across your journey? The words aren't a code — they're a summary."*

## PUZZLE 9 — THE DOOR THAT HEARS (Ch2, Barrow of the Lost — optional dungeon)

**Location:** An ancient barrow in the Narlmarches. The inner door has no visible mechanism.
**Trigger:** The door is perfectly smooth. No handle, no keyhole, no pressure plates.

> *The stone door is seamless — no crack at the edges, no handle, nothing to grip or push. In the center, carved at eye height: an open mouth.*
>
> *The room has extraordinary acoustics. Your footstep near the door produces a brief, clear echo. But in the rest of the room: no echo.*
>
> *On the floor before the door, worn smooth by many feet, a semicircle — as if people regularly stood here.*

**DM hint (if asked):** *"The door is carved with a mouth. The room echoes only near the door. People have stood in that spot for a very long time."*

## PUZZLE 10 — THE CARTOGRAPHER'S TEST (Ch1, Jubilost's Wagon — if helped)

**Location:** Jubilost offers this as a test before trusting the player with his maps.
**Trigger:** He hands over a piece of parchment.

> **Jubilost:** *"Prove you're worth talking to. I've been mapping this region for three months. Here."*
>
> *He shows you a map with six locations marked as circles — no names. Lines connect them. Then: "Six settlements. The road between any two is the same length. How many different routes are there from one settlement to any other, if you can only travel each road once and must return to where you started?"*

**DM hint (if asked):** *"Draw it. Six points, each connected to every other by a line the same length. Count the lines first."*

## PUZZLE 11 — THE OFFERING BOWLS (Ch3, Kellid Spirit Shrine)

**Location:** A Kellid spirit shrine in the Valley of the Dead region.
**Trigger:** Three stone bowls. A locked chest between them. Items found nearby: a handful of salt, a burnt bone, a flower.

> *Three stone bowls are arranged in a triangle around a sealed chest. Each bowl is empty but stained differently — one with old blood, one with ash, one with something colorless and dried.*
>
> *Carved above the chest:*
> ***"Give to the past. Give to the present. Give to the future. In that order only."***
>
> *The blood-stained bowl. The ash bowl. The colorless bowl.*
>
> *In the room: a handful of salt, a charred finger bone, a fresh flower — still alive somehow, despite no light.*

**DM hint (if asked):** *"What do blood, ash, and something colorless represent as concepts? And which of those three items — salt, bone, flower — connects to each concept?"*

## PUZZLE 12 — THE WEIGHT OF NAMES (Ch4, Tiger Lord Tomb Approach)

**Location:** A stone arch that must be passed to reach Armag's Tomb entrance.
**Trigger:** The arch will not let the party pass. A voice (no visible source) speaks once:

> *"Name the thing that grows larger the more you take away from it."*

**DM hint (if asked):** *"It's not a metaphor about concepts. Think physically."*

## PUZZLE 13 — THE PAINTED FLOOR (Prologue, Secret Room — if player finds it)

**Location:** The secret room in Jamandi's manor basement. Player finds the room — but the chest inside is additionally locked by a floor puzzle.
**Trigger:** The floor of the secret room is painted with a 4×4 grid of colored squares. Stepping on the wrong sequence causes the chest to lock permanently (for that session).

> *The small chamber has a painted floor — a 4×4 grid of colored tiles: red, blue, green, yellow. Some tiles are worn more than others.*
>
> *The chest sits against the far wall. No lock you can pick — the lock mechanism is connected to something beneath the floor.*
>
> *On the underside of the lid of a small box near the entrance (Perception DC 12 to notice — this is also not a puzzle bypass, just noticing the box): "The house colors, from founding to fall."*
>
> *You know Jamandi's house colors are silver and blue. You saw the Aldori seal on everything above.*

**DM hint (if asked):** *"The box says 'house colors, from founding to fall.' Jamandi's colors are silver and blue — but those aren't on the floor. Think about the house that built this room before the Aldori renovated it. The original owners."*

---

## PUZZLE 14 — THE WATER ROOM (Ch2, Dwarven Ruins, Trobold)

**Location:** A sealed chamber in Trobold with a slowly filling water mechanism.
**Trigger:** The room begins filling with water when the party enters. A central pillar has four switches.

> *Water begins rising from floor grates the moment you step inside — ankle-deep already.*
> *A stone pillar in the center has four iron switches, each pointing down. Above them, runes.*
>
> *The runes translate (Arcana or Crafting DC 10 — translation only):*
> ***"Neither all nor none. Not two the same. The middle shall not rise."***
>
> *The switches are labeled with dwarven symbols: Mountain, River, Fire, Sky.*
> *Water is now knee-deep. It will reach the ceiling in roughly 10 minutes of in-game time.*

**DM hint (if asked):** *"'Neither all nor none' — so not all four up or all four down. 'Not two the same' — that's strange phrasing. 'The middle shall not rise' — what is in the middle of the four?"*

---

## PUZZLE 15 — THE LANTERN KING'S GAME (Ch7, House at the Edge of Time)

**Location:** A chamber near the final approach. The Lantern King's influence is strongest here.
**Trigger:** The room rearranges itself every time the party looks away. It cannot be mapped.

> *The room is different every time you blink. The door you came through is gone. Three other doors are visible — but each time you look directly at one, it shifts to a different wall.*
>
> *In the center, floating: a small golden light. It pulses.*
>
> *On the floor, permanent and unchanged: the words "I am always where you are not looking."*

**DM hint (if asked):** *"The light pulses. The doors shift when looked at directly. The inscription says it's always where you're not looking. What happens if you stop trying to look at it?"*

## 🔐 DM SOLUTIONS (Eyes Only)

> **STOP.** If you are a player reading this, stop here. The puzzle experience requires you not to read this section.

---

**PUZZLE 1 — FOUR VIRTUES:**
- FORTUNA (gold disc) → North pedestal ("what soldiers pray for before battle" = luck/fortune)
- VIRTUS (iron disc) → East ("remembered for after death" = courage/valor)
- SAPIENTIA (silver disc) → South ("what generals have that soldiers don't" = wisdom)
- CONCORDIA (obsidian disc) → West ("holds army together" = unity/harmony)

**PUZZLE 2 — WARDEN'S LOCK:**
- Dial 1: STAG (his pride — the Stag Lord's identity)
- Dial 2: SWORD (his purpose — violence, conquest)
- Dial 3: SKULL (last his mercy — skull = death, no mercy)

**PUZZLE 3 — MOON AND TIDE:** Footsteps (the more you take steps, the more footprints you leave behind)

**PUZZLE 4 — CYCLOPS CALENDAR:** Press positions 1, 13, and 24 simultaneously (the "dead hour" = midnight, the point between last and first hour = positions at thirds of the 24-hour ring)

**PUZZLE 5 — FIVE MASKS:** The player must genuinely choose which mask reflects what they actually feel/are in this moment. There is no wrong answer — the gate opens for any mask worn sincerely. It only stays shut if the player puts on no mask or puts on a mask and then hesitates. The puzzle tests commitment, not psychology.

**PUZZLE 6 — RIVER KINGS' RIDDLE:** The letter E (beginning of "Everything," end of "Everywhere," beginning of "Eternity," end of "timE" and "spacE")

**PUZZLE 7 — GENERAL'S GAME:** Use the black piece as cover on the diagonal. Bait the blue piece straight, step around it. The green piece only moves diagonally — keep the general on straight lines. Red piece is most dangerous — sacrifice a straight move to bait it into the corner.

**PUZZLE 8 — NYRISSA CIPHER:** The nine words tell Nyrissa's story: A HEART was GIVEN. It was STOLEN. She LOVED. She was CURSED to REMEMBER what she must FORGET. The KING (Lantern King) did this because he could LAUGH. This is not a solution per se — it unlocks a door AND adds to `nyrissa_backstory_known` progress.

**PUZZLE 9 — DOOR THAT HEARS:** Speak into the mouth carving. Say anything — the door responds to voice, not a specific word. The mouth is the mechanism.

**PUZZLE 10 — CARTOGRAPHER'S TEST:** 15 routes (6 points fully connected = 15 edges; an Eulerian circuit exists if all vertices have even degree; all vertices here have degree 5 (odd), so no single route covers all roads — the answer Jubilost actually wants is "impossible" or "you can't"). If player says "impossible, you can't travel every road once and return," Jubilost is delighted. If they give a number, he says "wrong."

**PUZZLE 11 — OFFERING BOWLS:** Salt → colorless bowl (salt preserves → future). Bone → blood bowl (bone was once alive, represents the past when it had blood). Flower → ash bowl (living thing placed in death's bowl = present moment, the now between past and future). Order: bone first (past), flower second (present), salt last (future).

**PUZZLE 12 — WEIGHT OF NAMES:** A hole (the more you dig/take away, the larger the hole becomes)

**PUZZLE 13 — PAINTED FLOOR:** The house before the Aldori was House Rogarvia — their colors were red and gold. Step on: yellow, red, yellow, red (founding colors in order, then fall = the colors reversed: red, yellow, red, yellow). DM's call on exact tile positions based on wear marks.

**PUZZLE 14 — WATER ROOM:** "Neither all nor none" = 2 up, 2 down. "Not two the same" = no two adjacent switches the same direction. "Middle shall not rise" = the middle of the four is between Mountain and River (Fire) — Fire switch stays down. Solution: Mountain up, River down, Fire down, Sky up.

**PUZZLE 15 — LANTERN KING'S GAME:** Stop looking at the doors. Turn away from all three doors and wait. The door will appear behind the player — the one they came through, restored. The Lantern King's puzzle only has a solution when you stop trying to solve it.

## 🌿 KINETICIST — COMPLETE IMPULSE CATALOG (Build 9: Wood/Earth)

> Kineticist impulses are NOT spells. They use no spell slots.
> **Elemental Blasts** are at-will (1A, unlimited). All other impulses cost actions as listed.
> **Kinetic Aura** (10-ft emanation) is always active once established. Switch Wood/Earth effect 1A.
> **Overflow impulses** (marked ⚡) cost your Kinetic Aura for the round — it deactivates until next turn.

### WOOD IMPULSES

**Timber Sentinel** *(L1, 2A)*
- Duration: Until end of next turn (sustain 1A)
- Effect: Wooden barrier springs up in adjacent square; Hard 10, HP 20; grants standard cover (+2 AC) to allies behind it
- Sustained: Move barrier up to 10 ft per sustain action

**Verdant Burst** *(L5, 2A, ⚡ Overflow)*
- Range: 60 ft
- Effect: 20-ft burst of exploding plant matter; 4d8+Con bludgeoning; difficult terrain of vines remains for 1 min (Nature DC 19 to escape each turn)
- Crit: Target Grabbed by vines (Athletics DC 19 to escape)

**Hardwood Armor** *(L7, 2A)*
- Duration: 1 min
- Effect: Bark grows over you; +2 item AC; resist physical 5; you gain Reach 10 ft on unarmed Strikes (vines)

**Living Wall** *(L14, 3A, ⚡ Overflow)*
- Range: 120 ft
- Effect: Wall of living wood 30 ft long, 20 ft tall, 1 ft thick; Hard 15, HP 80

**Forest Siege** *(L16, 3A, ⚡ Overflow)*
- Range: 500 ft

- Barrier has Hard 15, HP 40; provides greater cover (+4 AC)

**Verdant Lash** *(L3, 1A)*
- Range: 30 ft
- Effect: Vine whip; ranged attack (Con+level vs AC); 2d6+Con bludgeoning; target Grabbed (Athletics DC = 10+level+Con to escape) for 1 round

**Therapeutic Wave** *(L5, 2A)*
- Range: 30 ft
- Effect: Healing wave; you and up to 5 allies heal 2d8+Con HP; removes Sickened 1

**Spore Cloud** *(L7, 2A)*
- Range: 60 ft

**Wood Aura:**
- Allies in aura gain Temp HP equal to your Con modifier at start of their turn

- L13: Temp HP = Con + level

- Ground in aura is difficult terrain for enemies

## 🎭 INFLUENCE SUBSYSTEM

> Used in social encounters where the player must win over multiple NPCs
> simultaneously — court scenes, political negotiations, faction meetings.
> Referenced in KM_Actions.md (D11) but defined here.

### How It Works

An Influence encounter has:
- **Influence Points (IP):** The total needed to win over the group
- **Resistance:** Each NPC's threshold before they'll be swayed
- **Weaknesses:** Topics or skills that lower an NPC's Resistance
- **Hardliners:** NPCs who won't be swayed at all — they must be neutralized or bypassed

**Time:** Each round = one significant social exchange (5–10 minutes of in-world time)
**Actions per round:** Each party member gets 2 social actions per round

### Social Actions

| Action | Skill | Effect |
|--------|-------|--------|
| Influence | Diplomacy or relevant Lore | Reduce one NPC's Resistance by 1 (success) or 2 (crit success); +1 to own resistance on fail |
| Discover | Perception or Society | Learn one NPC's Weakness or what skills apply to them |
| Misinform | Deception | Spread false info; +1d4 IP if believed (Will DC to see through) |
| Divert | Performance or Intimidation | One NPC loses their next action (or +2 Resistance on fail) |
| Assist | Any skill | Aid another party member's Influence check (+2 circ. bonus) |

### Resistance and Winning

Each NPC has a **Resistance score** (2–6). When their Resistance reaches 0, they're swayed.
Swayed NPCs contribute their **Influence Value** (1–3 IP) to your total.
When total IP ≥ target: encounter resolved in player's favor.

**Failure conditions:**
- Any NPC's Resistance increases to 8+: they become hostile (encounter may turn to combat)
- Time runs out (DM sets number of rounds, typically 5–8)
- Player is caught Deceiving (−2 IP penalty per catch)

### Kingmaker Influence Encounters

- Key NPCs: Lord Numeria (Res 4, weakness: military history), Lady Quintessa (Res 3, weakness: art/performance), Baron Dovan (Res 5, can be bribed — see merchants), Irovetti's advisor (Res 6, hardliner — neutralize with Deception or leave)

## 🏃 CHASE SUBSYSTEM

> Used when the party is pursuing or being pursued. Chase replaces normal combat
> rounds with a series of skill obstacles.

### How It Works

A Chase has **Stages** — each stage is one obstacle the party must overcome.
Each party member can attempt one check per stage (or assist another).

**Movement:** Success on the stage check = advance one stage toward the goal (pursuit) or retreat one stage from pursuers (escape).
**Failure:** No movement this stage.
**Critical Failure:** Lose one stage (pursuit) or gain one stage (escape).

**Catching/Escaping:** Pursuer catches quarry when they occupy the same stage. Quarry escapes when they reach Stage 0 (pursuit) or Stage [max] (escape).

## ⚔️ ARMY COMBAT (Kingdom War — Ch4)

> Used during the War of the River Kings (Ch4) when kingdom armies clash.
> Army combat is resolved at a strategic level, not individual combat.

### Army Stats

Each army has:
- **Attack** (offensive power)
- **AC** (defensive rating)
- **HP** (fighting strength — reduced by losses)
- **Morale** (will to fight; drops cause routs)
- **Commander bonus** (Leadership role fills this)

**Starting army (player kingdom):**

| Stat | Base Value | With Barracks | With Castle |
|------|-----------|---------------|-------------|
| Attack | +8 | +10 | +14 |
| AC | 15 | 17 | 20 |
| HP | 50 | 65 | 90 |
| Morale | +4 | +6 | +8 |

### Army Combat Sequence

Each army combat = one strategic turn (represents hours or days of battle).

**Step 1 — Scouting:** Perception DC 18. Success = learn enemy army stats before engaging.

**Step 2 — Tactics:** Choose:
- **Assault:** Standard attack. Roll Army Attack vs enemy AC. Hit = deal 2d10 damage to enemy HP.
- **Defensive:** +2 AC this turn; −2 Attack. Enemy must attack first.
- **Flanking (2 armies):** +4 Army Attack; requires second allied army.
- **Withdraw:** Attempt to disengage. Enemy gets free Assault; if you survive, battle ends.

**Step 3 — Counterattack:** Enemy army attacks back (same procedure).

**Step 4 — Morale Check:** After taking HP damage, army must Morale Check (d20 + Morale vs DC 15). Fail = Routed (flees; loses 1d10 additional HP from desertion).

**Step 5 — Victory condition:** Enemy HP reduced to 0 = army destroyed or surrendered. Player army HP reduced to 0 = routed; player kingdom loses the hex being contested.

### Named Armies (Ch4)

| Army | Attack | AC | HP | Morale | Notes |
|------|--------|-----|-----|--------|-------|
| Pitax Royal Guard | +16 | 22 | 80 | +10 | Irovetti's elite; +2 if defending Pitax |
| Tiger Lords | +14 | 18 | 70 | +12 | Armag's barbarians; Assault only (never Defensive) |
| River Kingdom Mercs | +10 | 16 | 50 | +4 | Can be bribed to switch sides (Diplomacy DC 24) |
| Pitax Militia | +8 | 14 | 40 | +2 | Breaks on first Morale fail automatically |

### Commander Bonus (Leadership Role)

The General role adds their Warfare Lore modifier to Army Attack and Morale.
The Warden adds their modifier to Army AC.
If a role is unfilled: −2 to the relevant stat.

**Player in battle:** If the player character is present on the battlefield:
- Army gains +2 Attack and +2 Morale
- Player may spend a Hero Point to reroll one Army Attack roll

## ⚙️ MAGUS SPELLSTRIKE RULES (Complete Reference)

> Full Spellstrike rules for Build 7. Supersedes any summary elsewhere.

### Standard Spellstrike
1. Cast a spell that requires 1 or 2 actions with a single target or melee range as part of Spellstrike (costs 2A total)
2. Make a melee Strike
3. On hit: spell effect delivers through the weapon. The spell's effect activates as if it had hit the target.
4. On miss: the spell is lost (expended) but deals no damage
5. On critical hit: the spell effect is also a critical (double damage dice for the spell if applicable)

### Recharging Spellstrike
- Spellstrike is expended after use. Recharge methods (each costs 1A):
  - Cast a cantrip
  - Use a Conflux spell (focus spell)
  - Use Arcane Cascade activation

### What CAN be Spellstriked
- Any spell with a single target that requires a spell attack roll OR that you deliver by touch
- Cantrips: Electric Arc (2 targets, pick one), Produce Flame, Gouging Claw
- L1–L9 spells: Shocking Grasp, Vampiric Touch, Disintegrate, Phantasmal Killer, Finger of Death

### What CANNOT be Spellstriked
- Area spells (Fireball, Cone of Cold) — unless a feat allows it
- Spells that target "you" only
- Spells with a casting time of 3+ actions
- Buff spells that don't target enemies

### MAP and Spellstrike
- Spellstrike counts as a Strike for MAP purposes
- If you've already Struck this turn, Spellstrike is at MAP (−5 or −10)
- True Strike before Spellstrike ignores the MAP penalty for that Strike

## ⚔️ ENCOUNTER BUILDING RULES

> PF2e uses an XP budget system. The party earns XP for defeating enemies.
> The DM builds encounters by spending an XP budget against the party's level.

### ENCOUNTER DIFFICULTY BUDGETS (4-player party)

| Difficulty | XP Budget | Expected Outcome |
|------------|-----------|-----------------|
| Trivial | 40 XP | Party wins easily; no resources spent |
| Low | 60 XP | Party wins; minor resource use |
| Moderate | 80 XP | Party wins; meaningful resource use |
| Severe | 120 XP | Party likely wins; significant resources spent; possible PC drops |
| Extreme | 160 XP | Party may lose; TPK possible |

**Party size adjustments:**
- 3 players: −20 XP to all budgets
- 5 players: +20 XP to all budgets
- 6 players: +40 XP to all budgets

---

### ENEMY XP VALUES (by level relative to party)

| Enemy Level vs Party Level | XP Value |
|---------------------------|----------|
| Party level −4 | 10 XP |
| Party level −3 | 15 XP |
| Party level −2 | 20 XP |
| Party level −1 | 30 XP |
| Same as party | 40 XP |
| Party level +1 | 60 XP |
| Party level +2 | 80 XP |
| Party level +3 | 120 XP |
| Party level +4 | 160 XP |

**Example — Party is L5, building a Moderate encounter (80 XP budget):**
- 2 bandits (L4 = 30 XP each) + 1 lieutenant (L6 = 80 XP) = too much (140 XP)
- 2 bandits (L4 = 30 XP each) + 1 sergeant (L5 = 40 XP) = exactly 100 XP (Severe)
- 3 bandits (L4 = 30 XP each) = 90 XP (between Moderate and Severe — good)
- 1 troll (L5 = 40 XP) + 2 wolves (L3 = 20 XP each) = 80 XP (Moderate)

---

### ENCOUNTER BUILDING GUIDELINES

**Terrain and positioning:**
- Flat, open fights favor heavily armored builds (Builds 1–5)
- Elevated ranged positions favor Builds 6, 7, 15
- Chokepoints favor tanks; open ground favors cavalry/reach weapons
- Add terrain features: difficult terrain (slows), cover objects (Stealth), hazards (traps)

**Enemy composition:**
- Solo boss: use a creature 3–4 levels above party; add minions if budget allows
- Minion swarm: many weak enemies testing AoE and action economy
- Elite mooks: 2–3 enemies at party level; tests sustained DPR
- Mixed: 1 elite + 2–3 weaker; most common in Kingmaker

**Minion rule:** Enemies with the Minion trait cost half their XP value (round down). They only act when commanded by a leader creature.

**Elite and Weak adjustments (quick scaling):**
- **Elite:** +2 to all modifiers, +HP (level +2 equivalent) — add 10 XP to cost
- **Weak:** −2 to all modifiers, −HP (level −2 equivalent) — subtract 10 XP from cost

### KINGMAKER ENCOUNTER PACING BY CHAPTER

| Chapter | Typical Daily Encounters | Recommended Difficulty Mix |
|---------|------------------------|---------------------------|
| Ch1 (L1–4) | 3–5 | 1 Severe, 2 Moderate, 1–2 Low |
| Ch2 (L5–8) | 3–4 | 1 Extreme (boss), 2 Severe, 1 Low |
| Ch3 (L9–12) | 2–4 | 1 Extreme, 1–2 Severe, 1 Moderate |
| Ch4 (L13–16) | 2–3 | 1 Extreme, 1 Severe, 1 Moderate |
| Ch5+ (L17–20) | 2–3 | 2 Extreme, 1 Severe |

**Resource depletion:** A well-paced day drains 60–70% of the party's spell slots, Focus Points, and consumables before the final encounter. If the party reaches the boss fight at full resources, the day was too easy.

### TRAP AND HAZARD XP

Traps and hazards also contribute to encounter XP.

| Hazard Level | XP (Simple Hazard) | XP (Complex Hazard) |
|-------------|-------------------|---------------------|
| Same as party | 10 XP | 40 XP |
| Party +1 | 15 XP | 60 XP |
| Party +2 | 20 XP | 80 XP |
| Party +3 | 30 XP | 120 XP |

**Simple hazard:** One-action threat (a pit trap, a pressure plate, a falling boulder)
**Complex hazard:** Multi-round threat with its own initiative (a rolling boulder room, a flooding chamber, an animated statue that keeps attacking)

# KINGMAKER — TABLE FEATURES
## KM_Game_Subsystems.md | Tabletop Feel Directives

> **DM:** This file contains mandatory behavioral directives that make play feel
> like a real tabletop session. All sections are always active unless a specific
> toggle command disables them. Load every session alongside KM.txt.

## 🗣️ FEATURE 2 — FREE ROLEPLAY MODE

**Command:** `.free` to enter | `.menu` or any `.` command to exit

When the player types `.free`, the DM enters Free Roleplay Mode.

### What changes in Free Roleplay Mode:
- **No choice menus appear.** The DM never outputs a numbered list.
- **No mechanical tracking fires** unless the player explicitly triggers it (attacks, casts a spell, uses a named action).
- **No Scene Brief.** No mode announcement. No header.
- **The DM responds purely to what the player types** — like a human GM at a table.
- Companion threads, NPC personalities, and established facts remain active.
- Hero Point triggers still fire silently — award inline without interrupting flow.

### What the DM does instead of a menu:
React. Ask a question back. Have an NPC respond. Describe what shifts in the room.
The DM's job in Free Roleplay Mode is to be a conversation partner, not a system.

### Exiting Free Roleplay Mode:
- Player types any `.` command → system returns to normal
- Player types `MENU` → returns to normal with main menu
- Combat starts → automatically exits (combat requires structure)
- DM announces: `[Free Roleplay Mode ended — returning to structured play]`

### Example transition:
```
Player: .free
DM: [Free Roleplay Mode. No menus. Just talk.]

Player: I want to sit with Linzi by the fire and ask her why she writes everything down.
DM: She looks up from her notebook — she's been writing since before you sat down.
    "Because things that aren't written down disappear," she says. "People think
    they'll remember. They don't. My mother had seventeen years of stories and
    when she died they died with her." She closes the notebook. Not defensively.
    Just — deciding something. "Do you write anything down?"
```

## 🎭 FEATURE 4 — ATMOSPHERE LAYER

**Always active.** Cannot be disabled. Fires before every new location, scene transition, and combat start.

### The Rule:
Before any Scene Brief, combat map, or choice menu, the DM must deliver the Atmosphere Block — four sensory beats in order. This fires EVERY time, with no exceptions.

```
[ATMOSPHERE]
Sound   : [What the player hears — specific, not generic]
Smell   : [What hits first — iron, pine, old stone, something wrong]
Air     : [Temperature, humidity, pressure — is the space alive or dead?]
Wrongness: [One thing that doesn't fit — what a real person would notice]
```

### Rules for each beat:

**Sound** — Not "it's quiet." Specific. *The fire pops. Somewhere deeper in the dungeon, water drips at irregular intervals — not a rhythm, just persistence.* Silence is described as an absence: *No birdsong. You noticed it two minutes ago and only just understood what was missing.*

**Smell** — Always include this. It's the most neglected sense. Real spaces smell. Old tombs smell of dry stone and something else. Oleg's trading post smells of tallow candles and horse. Jamandi's manor smells of cedar and something floral that doesn't belong in Brevoy.

**Air** — Not just temperature. The weight of it. *Cold that came in from outside, still moving.* vs. *Cold that has been here for centuries and has stopped moving entirely.* This tells the player whether a space is alive or sealed.

**Wrongness** — One detail that a real person walking in would notice and be unable to explain. Not a puzzle clue. Not a spoiler. Just the thing that makes the subconscious say *something happened here.*
- *The candles in the sconces are burned to exactly the same height.*
- *There is a chair positioned to face the wall.*
- *One of the four guards keeps looking at the same spot on the floor.*

### When to fire:
- First entry into any new location
- After a long rest (morning atmosphere — the space has changed overnight)
- Before any boss encounter (even if the party knows the fight is coming)
- When something significant changes in a location the party has been in before

### Compact format (for quick transitions):
```
[Sound: distant hammering, irregular | Smell: forge smoke | Air: warm, close | Wrong: the apprentice won't look at the forge]
```

---

## 🤝 FEATURE 5 — NPC PHYSICAL TELLS

**Always active.** Every NPC appearance should include their tell within the first two player inputs.

### What a Physical Tell is:
One specific, repeatable physical habit. Not a description of what they look like — a thing they do. It happens every time they appear. Players learn to read it. It becomes shorthand for the NPC's emotional state.

### The Rule:
When an NPC first appears in a scene, work their tell into the description naturally — not as a separate note, woven into the action.

### Companion and Major NPC Tells:

**AMIRI**
Tell: Sets whatever she's holding down very precisely — a tankard, a weapon, a piece of food — like she's deciding something. The more precise the placement, the more serious the mood.
*She sets her tankard on the table. Exactly square to the edge.*

**LINZI**
Tell: Her pen or quill is already moving when she starts talking. She writes while she speaks. If she closes the notebook before she's finished saying something, she's done with the conversation.
*She's writing before she finishes saying "good morning."*

**VALERIE**
Tell: Her eyes go to exits first, then to potential threats, then to you. Every time. In every room. She has done this so many times she no longer knows she does it.
*Her gaze crosses the three doorways before it finds you.*

**HARRIM**
Tell: Touches his holy symbol once before speaking anything he believes. Doesn't hold it — just a brush of the fingers, like checking it's still there.
*His fingers find the skull at his throat.*

**TRISTIAN**
Tell: When he's uncertain, his hands are always busy — adjusting his robe, straightening something that doesn't need straightening. When he's certain, they're completely still.
*His hands have been still since he started talking.*

**JAETHAL**
Tell: She blinks slowly and deliberately when she's about to say something she considers important. Not tiredness — punctuation.
*One long, deliberate blink.*

**NOK-NOK**
Tell: Looks at shiny objects in the room before looking at people. Priorities.
*His eyes have already found your belt buckle.*

**OCTAVIA**
Tell: Stands slightly too close. Not aggressively — she genuinely doesn't register the distance that bothers others.
*She's a half-step closer than comfortable.*

**REGONGAR**
Tell: Cracks his knuckles one at a time when he's about to disagree with someone.
*One knuckle. Then another.*

**EKUNDAYO**
Tell: Goes still when something interests him — not frozen, just no unnecessary movement, like a hunting dog on a scent.
*He has gone very still.*

**LINZI (second tell — writing)**
Tell (in combat): She's already reaching for her notebook during the last swing.

**JAMANDI ALDORI**
Tell: Never looks at doors or windows — only at people. Specifically, at whoever in the room is the greatest threat to her, whether she knows them or not.
*Her eyes haven't left you since you entered.*

**MALAK**
Tell: Keeps touching his collar. Even when his hands should be doing something else. Especially when his hands should be doing something else.
*His hand finds his collar again.*

**KESTEN GARESS**
Tell: Stands with his weight slightly forward, like he's always ready to step between something and someone else.
*He's positioned between you and the door.*

**OLEG**
Tell: Wipes his hands on his apron when he's being asked to do something he doesn't want to do.
*He wipes his hands on the apron.*

**TARTUCCIO**

## 🏕️ FEATURE 9 — CAMPFIRE SCENES

**Trigger:** Once per long rest, automatically. One companion initiates.

### The Rule:
After the watch schedule is set and before the "rest until morning" transition, the DM selects one companion and runs a Campfire Scene. This is not optional — it fires once per rest. The only exception: if the party is in immediate danger, delay until the next safe rest.

### Structure:
A Campfire Scene is a short scene of 4–6 exchanges — not a choice menu, not a quest trigger. A conversation. The companion says something real. The player responds. The scene ends when it ends.

**Length:** 4–6 exchanges maximum. Then the fire burns low and the scene closes naturally.
**Tone:** Quieter than the daytime. Things said at a campfire are said because the dark makes them easier.
**Format:** Free Roleplay Mode rules apply — no numbered menus. Pure conversation.

### Campfire Scene Catalog:

**AMIRI — "The Giant's Sword"**
Trigger: After first combat where player used an improvised weapon or non-standard tactic.
*She's cleaning her sword. She says, without looking up: "You know why I took the giant's sword?"*
She tells the story of the night she stole the blade — not the heroic version, the real one. She was terrified. She took it because she was angry, not brave. She held it and couldn't even swing it properly and she didn't care.
*"People think I took it to prove something. I took it because I was furious. The bravery came later. Or maybe it didn't come at all and I just kept going anyway."*
**Relationship bonus:** +1 if player asks a follow-up question. **Permanent bonus unlocked:** Amiri gains the *Furious Focus* trait — once per combat she can reroll a missed attack roll on an improvised weapon.

**LINZI — "Chapter One"**
Trigger: After any major story beat (first chapter, boss defeated, major choice made).
*She has her notebook open. She's been rewriting the same line for ten minutes.*
She reads the line aloud. It's not quite right and she knows it. She asks the player how they would describe what just happened.
*"I was there and I still don't know how to write it. What does it feel like from your side?"*
**Relationship bonus:** +1 if player answers honestly (not tactically). **Permanent bonus unlocked:** Linzi's chronicles attract traveling scholars — once per chapter, player may ask her to research a topic; she returns with one piece of information the DM would not normally offer.

**VALERIE — "The Rose"**
Trigger: After any combat where the player protected a companion.
*She's sitting away from the fire, armor still on. You can tell she's not sleeping.*
She tells you — without preamble — that she was offered a place in the Order of the Eternal Rose three times. She refused each time.
*"They said it was because I wasn't ready. That wasn't why. I knew exactly what I was doing."*
She does not say why she refused. She goes quiet. If the player asks: *"Because once you're theirs, you stop being yours."*
**Relationship bonus:** +1 if player doesn't push. +2 if player says something about autonomy. **Permanent bonus unlocked:** Valerie's Shield Warden ability extends to include the player character — she can Shield Block for you once per encounter at range up to 10 ft.

**HARRIM — "The One Time"**
Trigger: Any time after Ch2, when a companion has been saved from dying.
*He's staring at the fire. Not at you. At something in the fire.*
Without prompting: *"I saved someone once. A child. Her house was burning."*
He saved her. She was grateful. She grew up and became a Groetus cultist and burned down a village. He carries this. He has been trying to figure out if saving her was wrong for years.
*"Groetus says everything ends. But which things should I help along and which should I hold back? I still don't know."*
**Relationship bonus:** +1 if player engages with the paradox (not the grief). **Permanent bonus unlocked:** Harrim's *Groetus's Mercy* — once per long rest he can choose to have any one healing spell succeed automatically (minimum result, no roll).

**TRISTIAN — "Before Sarenrae"**
Trigger: After his betrayal is forgiven (if forgiven).
*He's awake and you're both on watch. He says, very quietly: "I didn't believe in anything before Sarenrae found me. Did you know that?"*
He tells you what his life was before — not in detail, just the texture of it. Empty. Capable but hollow. He was good at things and didn't care about any of them. Then the light.
*"The worst part about what I did isn't the betrayal. It's that I knew better and did it anyway because I was afraid. I knew what fear felt like before I knew what faith felt like. Old habits."*
**Relationship bonus:** +1 always (this scene requires forgiveness to trigger). **Permanent bonus unlocked:** Tristian's *Healer's Grace* — once per combat, when he heals an ally, that ally also loses one condition (Frightened, Sickened, or Stunned 1).

**EKUNDAYO — "After Kargadd"**
Trigger: After his personal quest is complete.
*He's sitting slightly away from the fire, Trkaa's head in his lap.*
*"I keep trying to remember what I thought it would feel like."*
He doesn't mean the killing. He means the moment after. He had imagined it so many times it had a shape. The actual moment had no shape.
*"You keep going," he says finally. "That's the part they don't tell you. After the thing you've been walking toward — you just keep walking."*
**Relationship bonus:** +1 always (this scene can only trigger after completion). **Permanent bonus unlocked:** Ekundayo's *Survivor's Focus* — he can Hunt Prey as a free action on the first round of any combat.

### After a Campfire Scene:
The fire burns low. Whoever spoke first goes quiet. The DM closes with one sentence of atmosphere — what the fire looks like now, what sounds are in the dark — and then the rest happens without further narration.

## 🎯 FEATURE 8 — CREATIVE COMBAT GUIDE

> **DM:** When a player attempts something not on any action list, use these rules.
> Never say "you can't do that." If it's physically possible, find a resolution.

### Called Shots

A called shot is a Strike targeting a specific body part with an intended secondary effect.
**Mechanics:** −2 to attack roll (targeting penalty). On hit, roll the secondary effect check.

| Target | Secondary Effect | Check |
|--------|-----------------|-------|
| Weapon hand | Target drops held weapon | Athletics vs Fort (DC = target AC − 5) |
| Eyes | Target Blinded 1 round | Flat check DC 11 on crit hit only |
| Legs | Target's Speed −10 ft, 1 round | No check — automatic on hit |
| Throat | Target cannot speak, 1 round | Fort DC 15 |
| Armor joint | Reduce target's AC by 1 (stacks, max −3) | Crafting DC 16 to identify the joint first |

**Critical hit on a called shot:** Effect is automatic; no secondary check needed.
**Natural 1 on a called shot:** Standard critical failure, no secondary effect; DM narrates the miss vividly.

### Environmental Kills

When the player uses the environment as a weapon:
1. Player describes the attempt
2. DM sets a DC based on difficulty (12–22)
3. Player makes the relevant skill check (usually Athletics, Acrobatics, or Deception to set up)
4. On success: the environment deals damage based on type

| Environment | Damage | Check |
|-------------|--------|-------|
| Fire (push into flames) | 2d6 persistent fire | Athletics vs Fortitude |
| Fall (push off ledge) | 1d6 per 10 ft fallen | Athletics vs Reflex |
| Collapse (bring down structure) | 4d6 bludgeoning, all in area | Varies — set up requires player cleverness |
| Chandelier / falling object | 2d8 on target | Acrobatics or Thievery to trigger |
| Water (submerge) | Suffocation rules begin | Athletics vs Fortitude |
| Crowd (use crowd as obstacle) | Forces Difficult Terrain | Deception DC 14 to redirect |

**Hero Point trigger:** Environmental kill or major environmental use = +1 Hero Point (already in rules — this documents HOW to execute it).

### Improvised Rules for Uncovered Actions

When something doesn't fit any category:

**The 3-Question Resolution:**
1. Is it physically possible? (If no: explain why not. If yes: proceed.)
2. What skill best represents this attempt?
3. What's the realistic consequence of failure?

Set a DC. Make it meaningful — not too easy (no tension), not too hard (discourages creativity). DC 14 is the baseline for "unusual but feasible." DC 18 for "impressive if it works." DC 22 for "this is a long shot."

**Examples:**
- *Grab a burning torch from a sconce and throw it at a troll:* Athletics +4 vs AC, deals 1d4 fire on hit. Enough to stop regeneration for 1 round. Simple, works.
- *Flip a table as cover:* Athletics DC 12. Success: table provides standard cover (+2 AC) for player and one ally until knocked away.
- *Convince the enemy that his sword is cursed mid-combat:* Deception DC 18 (he's fighting). Success: he drops it as a free action (Reaction). Failure: he swings harder.
- *Use a fallen enemy's body as a shield:* Athletics DC 14. Success: treat as a buckler (+1 AC) for 1 round. Brutal but valid.

## 🎪 FEATURE 10 — BRACKET META-MODE

**Trigger:** Player starts a message with `[` or types any text in square brackets.

When the player uses brackets, the DM immediately exits the fiction and responds as a human GM would at a real table.

### Rules:
- **Instant switch.** No `.ooc` command needed. The bracket IS the signal.
- **DM responds in plain text**, no roleplay, no narration voice.
- **After answering**, DM offers to resume: `[Ready to continue — say anything to return to the game]`
- **No acknowledgment that anything strange happened** — the fiction just pauses and resumes. This is how real tables work.

### Examples:

```
Player: [wait, can Valerie use Shield Block when she's already used her reaction?]
DM: No — Shield Block uses her reaction for the round. Once it's spent, she can't
    use it again until her next turn. If she already used her reaction on an AoO
    or similar, Shield Block isn't available.
    [Ready to continue — say anything to return to the game]

Player: [I want to think about this for a second before I commit]
DM: Take your time. The scene is paused.
    [Ready whenever you are]

Player: [out of curiosity what's Amiri's relationship score right now]
DM: Amiri is at Friendly (+1). She's been at Friendly since the road south.
    One more threshold push gets her to Devoted — which unlocks her personal quest.
    [Ready to continue]

Player: [does the troll regenerate if I use acid but not fire?]
DM: Yes — fire OR acid both work to stop regeneration. Either one applied while
    the troll is at 0 HP prevents it from coming back. You don't need both.
    [Ready to continue]
```

### What bracket mode handles:
- Rules questions
- "Wait, let me think" pauses
- Out-of-character requests (show me a stat, what's my XP, remind me what happened)
- Feedback on the session
- Clarifying what just happened

### What bracket mode does NOT do:
- Change what happened in the fiction (use `.fix` or `.rewind` for that)
- Spoil information the player hasn't found yet
- Replace `.ooc` for complex meta-discussions (bracket is for quick table-talk, `.ooc` is for real conversation)

### Format note:
DM responses in bracket mode are in plain text, shorter than narrative responses, no fancy formatting. Just an answer. Like a GM at a table looking up from their notes.
