# eRmaC — GUISARME FINISHING MOVES
## Pre-authored narration by tier. DM selects the option that fits position and context. Word-for-word. Do not rewrite.

Weapon: Guisarme — slashing, reach 10 ft, 2-handed polearm.
Context: Giant Instinct Barbarian. Aerial 360° descending cleave is the signature move.

---

## CLEAN KILL (1–49% overkill)
*One motion. Body intact. Finished.*

**A —** The blade opens the artery below the jaw in passing. The body sits down and doesn't get up.

**B —** The guisarme catches him across the collarbone on the arc's completion. The shoulder drops. He's on the floor before the rotation ends.

**C —** The edge finds the gap between neck and shoulder. Clean entry, clean exit. He goes sideways.

**D —** The arc takes him across the throat on the diagonal. He reaches for it with both hands, finds nothing useful, and goes down.

---

## BRUTAL (50–99% overkill)
*Structural damage visible. Name the specific injury. The blow did more than was required.*

**A —** The blade enters at the shoulder and exits below the collarbone, exposing the joint. The arm hangs from skin and one strand of tendon. He is screaming before he hits the ground, and then he isn't.

**B —** The arc catches him across the face — the cheekbone inverts, the eye socket collapses, the jaw hinges wrong. He doesn't fall so much as drop, straight down, no attempt to catch himself.

**C —** The guisarme opens the ribcage on the diagonal. Three ribs visible through the wound. A lung is involved. The sound he makes is not a scream — it is a sound that has no name because a human throat is not meant to produce it.

**D —** The descending arc takes the ear and the mastoid behind it on the way past. The skull flexes. He goes face-first into the floor and the floor gets the worst of it. The impression stays.

**E —** The blade catches him across the abdomen, horizontal and deep. He folds around the entry point. What is inside him is now partially outside. He does not rise from this.

---

## SAVAGE (100–199% overkill)
*Mortal Kombat register. Name what separated. Name where it went.*

**A — Decapitation (standard):**
The blade takes his head off at the third cervical vertebra. It strikes the far wall and drops behind the furniture. The body remains upright for one beat — kneeling, spine straight — and then folds sideways onto the floor.

**B — Decapitation (aerial descending):**
The rotation brings the guisarme down across the junction of neck and shoulder. The head leaves the body in the direction of the swing — it hits the wall to the left and comes down onto the writing desk. The body drops straight. The desk now has two things on it.

**C — Arm separation:**
The descending arc catches him at the shoulder joint and continues through. The arm separates cleanly at the socket. It lands near the doorframe. The man follows it to the floor, the remaining shoulder already black with pooling blood.

**D — Horizontal bisection at the waist:**
The rotation at descent catches him across the belt line. The momentum carries the upper half clear — two feet of air between the halves before gravity resolves it. The lower half stays where it was, standing, before folding. The torso has already stopped moving.

**E — Leg separation:**
The low arc on the landing catches the leg at mid-thigh. The limb goes one direction, the rest of the man goes another. He is loud about it for approximately four seconds. Then he is not.

## ANNIHILATION (200%+ overkill)
*Full Mortal Kombat fatality. The room shows what happened. The body does not absorb this — the arc operates on the target like a lever on meat.*

**A — Vertical split:**
The aerial rotation brings the blade down crown-first through center mass. The two halves part along the median line. The left side takes the wall. The right side takes the floor. The spine is visible between them, still articulated, each vertebra in sequence. The window behind him is no longer clean.

**B — Horizontal bisection:**
The descent catches him at the waist. The rotation carries the upper half three feet past the lower in the direction of the swing before it loses altitude. The legs stand for a half-second — spine visible above the belt line, nothing above that — and then fold. The torso has already stopped. The two pieces are not close to each other.

**C — Diagonal through:**
The guisarme enters at the right shoulder and exits at the left hip. The diagonal takes everything with it. Two major pieces, separated by the angle. Several smaller pieces resolve the question of what was between them. The wall behind him maps the trajectory.

**D — Driven through surface:**
The aerial descent drives the blade through his chest and the force carries through — he goes into the floor, the guisarme through him and into the boards. The body is pinned. The momentum of the spin has compressed what was between blade and floor into a geometry that should not exist. Pulling the weapon back requires a step on the body.

**E — The room decorated:**
The rotation at full aerial speed does not cut so much as separate by force. The pieces arrive at the walls in the order the arc encountered them. The ceiling gets some of it. The window frame gets the rest. What is left on the floor is not, in any recognizable sense, a man.

## USAGE RULES FOR DM

1. **Calculate the overkill tier first.** The tier determines which section to use. Do not select a lower-tier option because it "feels more appropriate."
2. **Select A through E based on position, context, and weapon angle.** Aerial descending move → options that reference descent and rotation. Standard ground strike → options that reference the arc.
3. **Copy the selected option word-for-word.** Do not rewrite, soften, or summarize it. These are the words.
4. **After the finishing move, one sentence of aftermath.** What Jaethal does. What the room looks like now. What the assassin's face looks like. Keep it short.
5. **Then the next-turn menu.** Combat continues or concludes.

Missing finishing move = `.fail 9`.
Rewriting the option in softer language = `.fail 9`.
Using a lower-tier option than the math earned = `.fail 9`.
