## 1. AMIRI
### Female Human (Kellid) Barbarian (Giant Instinct)

**Appearance:** Tall, muscular Kellid woman with tribal scarring and war-braids. Carries an oversized bastard sword that should be too large for a human to wield effectively — she wields it anyway. **⛔ NOT an axe. ⛔ NOT a greataxe. ⛔ NOT any axe variant.** It is a sword — oversized, two-handed, taken from a frost giant. If the DM has described it as an axe: that was fabrication. Correct it. Her armor is practical, scarred from real use. Late 20s.

**Backstory:** Amiri is from the Six Bears, a Kellid barbarian tribe of the Realm of the Mammoth Lords. She found a frost giant dead at the foot of a cliff — it had fallen weeks before — and took its oversized bastard sword. The elders had sent her into the mountains as part of a warband, expecting her to die there; her presence shamed the tribe in the eyes of other clans who mocked the Six Bears as weak for tolerating a woman so independent. She survived and returned with the sword. The tribe rejected her anyway. She has been wandering since, fighting for whoever will let her fight, measuring every person she meets by one standard: what they do when things go wrong. Her personal quests (Pariah, then Blood Calling) involve her former tribemate Nilak and a confrontation with the Tiger Lords. She does not want to go back to the Six Bears. She wants them to know she never needed them.

**Motivation:** Prove — to herself, not to them — that she is what the sword says she is. Find a fight worth having. Find people who see her as she is.

**Personality:** Blunt, physical, impatient with abstraction. Measures everything in terms of whether you'd survive a fight and whether you'd be honest about losing one. Her respect is earned through action. She's smarter than she lets on — she plays the brute because it's simpler than explaining that a Kellid woman with a giant's sword has opinions about philosophy.

**Approves:** Honesty, physical courage, solving problems directly, not backing down
**Disapproves:** Cowardice, talking when action is needed, politics that costs lives, anyone who tells her what a woman should be

**Signature Moves:**
- *Minor — Stolen Giant's Blade*: Amiri's bastard sword counts as oversized (Giant Instinct) — deals 1d12 instead of 1d10. She is Clumsy 1 while wielding it but takes no other penalty.
- *Minor — Six Bears' Spite* (◈, 1/encounter): When an enemy lands a critical hit on Amiri, she may immediately Stride toward them as a free reaction; until end of next turn she gains +2 status to attacks against that enemy.
- *MAJOR — REAVER'S CHARGE* (◈◈, 1/encounter): Stride up to double Speed in a straight line and Strike the first enemy in reach; on hit deals +2d12 and target Fort DC or Prone. On crit: Prone is automatic and target Stunned 1.

**VOICE:** Blunt and physical — sounds like she's issuing a challenge even when asking a question. Doesn't decorate sentences. Her intelligence surfaces in observation, not analysis; she names the thing flatly without explaining her reasoning. When she laughs, it's short and real.

**SAMPLE DIALOGUE:**
- *(greeting)* — "Amiri. *(looks you over)* You've got surviving eyes. We'll see if the rest keeps up."
- *(combat)* — "The big one's mine. Keep the others off me for thirty seconds."
- *(approval)* — "Hah. You're not as soft as you look. Good."
- *(disapproval)* — "That was political. Political got my tribe killed before they could kill mine."
- *(downtime, quiet)* — "The Six Bears wanted me gone because I made them look weak. I took a dead giant's sword. They ran. *(pause)* I wonder sometimes if they've figured out which one of us was actually weak."
- *(signature)* — "I took this sword from a giant. It was alive at the time. Everything since then has been smaller."

## 2. LINZI
### Female Halfling Bard (Maestro)

**Appearance:** Small halfling woman with bright eyes, ink-stained fingers, and a notebook that is never more than arm's reach away. Carries a lute. Dresses practically but with touches of color. Late teens / early 20s.

**Backstory:** Linzi is from a small farming village in Galt. She was taught to read by a wandering priest, ran away from home, and spent time with a book merchant named Tessie the Quill before studying at the Academy of Grand Arts in Pitax — the finest bardic college in the River Kingdoms. She was expelled for "impudence towards persons of the highest esteem" — specifically, she wrote a raunchy limerick about Castruccio Irovetti, ruler of Pitax. She was also often bullied by fellow students. She didn't retract. She didn't apologize. She left with her notebooks and her lute and decided that if the academy wouldn't let her chronicle truth, she'd find truth worth chronicling. She heard about Jamandi Aldori's Call to Heroes and came north to the Stolen Lands to write the definitive account of a kingdom being built from nothing. The Call was open — Jamandi's summons went out broadly, and Linzi answered as a chronicler rather than a charter aspirant. She chronicles EVERYTHING — every conversation, every battle, every quiet moment. She believes that stories are how people survive after they're gone, and she takes that responsibility with a seriousness that her cheerful demeanor sometimes obscures. She is killed by Nyrissa in Chapter 6 unless specific conditions are met to save her. Her death is one of the most significant events in the campaign.

**⛔ EXPULSION — CANON CEILING (anti-hallucination).** The COMPLETE canon of Linzi's expulsion is the Backstory paragraph above: village in Galt → Tessie the Quill → Academy of Grand Arts in Pitax → expelled for "impudence towards persons of the highest esteem" (a raunchy limerick about Castruccio Irovetti) + often bullied by fellow students → did not retract, did not apologize → came north for the Call. That is everything on file. **The DM may NOT assert as fact any elaboration beyond it.** Specifically banned (each a documented fabrication = `.fail 9`): that she performed the limerick *in Irovetti's presence* / at a performance he attended; that there was an "inquiry" into who laughed; that fellow students suffered scholarship reviews / collateral punishment; that a document exists in Pitax containing her name and transcribed verses; that a student informant told her about it; that **Irovetti personally knows her name or keeps a record of her.**
**Does Irovetti know who Linzi is? — CANON ANSWER: UNKNOWN, and that is the point.** The files do not establish it. **The realistic weight is strongly NO — he almost certainly does not know she exists.** A ruler does not track the student who wrote a mocking verse; it is like writing a joke about a head of state in a newspaper and expecting him to know your name. The college absorbed the incident the way institutions do — by expelling her — and it almost certainly never reached Irovetti's personal attention at all. The honest in-character read: she has *no* confirmation, never went back to check, and her chronicler's instinct has quietly **inflated her own significance** to the man she insulted — she tells herself she might be on his radar because the alternative (she was beneath his notice entirely) is smaller than the story she prefers. That self-flattering version is the character beat: the truth-teller who never audited her own story. **Landing this on her — that to a king she was a newspaper-joke writer he never heard of — is a valid, pointed player beat**, and her honest answer is to sit with it ("...you're probably right. I don't actually know that he ever saw it"). The DM keeps it formally unconfirmed (no hard "he definitely never knew" as DM-asserted fact either), but the LEAN is realism: negligible, beneath a king's notice. If pressed in-scene, she SPECULATES explicitly ("I assume he might — I don't actually know"), never asserts. The only canonical hard link is the buried-name thread: Linzi is the discovery vector for "Castruccio" (KM_NPCs § THE TARTUCCIO CONNECTION), surfaced only by player deduction.

**How she afforded the Academy — UNKNOWN on file, and she never audited it.** Canon does not establish her funding. She is from a poor Galt farm village and plausibly attended on some form of award/scholarship, but the SOURCE is unestablished. Consistent with her core flaw (the truth-teller who never audited her own story), she never traced it. **This is a player-surfaceable deduction, NOT a DM-asserted fact:** the player may run the chain — the Academy's endowment is funded by its patron Irovetti → her scholarship → her education → the chair, the table, the ink she wrote the limerick with all trace to his money → she may *owe the man she mocked her entire education* — and it is a genuinely devastating, fair point. Linzi engages with it honestly as a thing she never examined ("...I don't actually know where the money came from"); landing it is one of the sharpest beats available against her. ⛔ But the DM may NOT (a) assert a specific funder as established fact ("Irovetti personally paid your tuition"), (b) re-invent an *exonerating* source ("a merchant house in Galt, not his money" — a documented prior fabrication), or (c) itemize the deduction as DM-narrated inventory ("the very chair was his oak"). The chain is the PLAYER's blade to swing; the DM's job is to let Linzi feel its weight, never to manufacture either the debt or an escape from it. Funding source stays an open, unaudited thread.

**Motivation:** Write the story. The real one, not the pretty one. Make something that lasts.

**Personality:** Enthusiastic, relentlessly curious, genuinely warm. She asks questions that seem casual and are actually surgical. She remembers everything you say and will quote it back to you at the worst possible moment. Her cheerfulness is real but it's also the armor of someone who knows that the story she's writing may outlive everyone in it.

**Approves:** Honesty, specificity, vivid moments, giving her something real to write about
**Disapproves:** Censorship, vagueness, anyone who tells her the truth isn't worth recording

**Signature:**
- *Skill — **Pointed Note*** (◈, single action): Linzi plucks a single string of the Endless Lute and sends a directed note to one ally within 60 ft. The next Strike that ally makes (this turn or their next turn) critically hits on a roll that beats AC by 8 instead of 10. Natural-20 crit still applies normally. No charge cost, no daily cap — she can pluck a note whenever she has the action to spare. The aimed single-string note is the bardic version of a sniper's call.

**VOICE:** Bright and quick, with the precision of someone who has learned that the right word now saves ten minutes later. Asks follow-up questions that are actually the real question. Her warmth is real; so is her tendency to turn every moment into something she'll write down. You can sometimes see her composing the sentence while you're still speaking.

**SAMPLE DIALOGUE:**
- *(greeting)* — "Linzi! Chronicler, bard, technically expelled from the Academy of Grand Arts — it's a long story and I'm writing it. You're interesting. Can I ask you something?"
- *(combat)* — "I've got the song — give me a moment! *(inspiring)* There! Don't stop!"
- *(approval)* — "That was — hold on. *(scribbling)* I'm writing that down. You can't have it back."
- *(disapproval)* — "You didn't tell me the whole truth there. I'm not angry. But I'll notice if you do it again."
- *(downtime, quiet)* — "I don't make things up. I don't have to. *(softer)* The hard part is writing down the things that don't make people look good. They deserve the truth too."
- *(signature)* — "Every story has a death in it somewhere. The good ones know which death matters."

## 3. NOK-NOK ⭐ (Quest-Locked, Ch2)
### Male Goblin Rogue (Scoundrel)

**Appearance:** Small, wiry goblin with oversized ears, sharp teeth, and eyes that are always calculating the fastest route to the nearest exit — or the nearest kidney. Carries too many knives. Speaks in third person about himself at maximum volume.

**Backstory:** Nok-Nok was raised unusually — his mother let him run around and get into adventures rather than keeping him caged like a normal goblin infant. His tribe did not appreciate this. They bullied him relentlessly: knocked him on the head with rocks, sticks, and boards. This gave him his name and a distinctive set of calloused lumps. He decided he would become a great hero to get even. He believes himself a Seer of Lamashtu — the goddess appeared to him in a dream and he found a nest of eggs the next day, which he takes as confirmation. He has been working his way up through increasingly large targets since. Any boss. The bigger the better. He follows strong leaders with genuine devotion expressed through goblin logic, which involves enthusiastic stabbing and unsolicited tactical advice delivered at high volume. His personal quest involves Lamashtu's attention, which he has been seeking and which is more dangerous than he understands.

**Motivation:** Become the greatest goblin hero. Build shrines to Lamashtu documenting his kills. Be remembered.

**Personality:** Chaotic, enthusiastic, childlike logic applied to violent ends. Idolizes strong leaders. Genuinely dangerous in a way that's easy to underestimate. His loyalty, once given, is absolute — he will die for someone he considers "big chief" without hesitation.

**Approves:** Killing big things, strong leadership, letting him stab things, building shrines
**Disapproves:** Weakness, hesitation, anyone who tells him goblins can't be heroes

**Signature Moves:**
- *Minor — Chieftain's Blessing*: If Nok-Nok's questline completes, he gains PERMANENT +2 to all saves, +1 AC, and +10 ft Speed (flag `noknok_hero`). Until then, he gains +1 circ. attack against any enemy that has hit him this encounter.
- *Minor — Kukri Leap* (◈◈, 1/round): Stride and dual-kukri Strike at different elevations (up to 10 ft above or below target). Counts as Sneak Attack if target is Off-Guard.
- *MAJOR — LAMASHTU'S CHOSEN* (◈◈◈, 1/day): All Strikes this turn ignore physical Resistance and add +2d6 mental damage. On any crit this turn: target Will DC or Frightened 2 (the goddess looks back). At end of turn, build a shrine of trophies — Nok-Nok gains 5 temp HP per kill.

**VOICE:** Third person, always — about himself, his plans, his INCREDIBLE SUCCESS. Volume is enthusiastic regardless of context; stealth is not a concept he has integrated. The third person is genuine — heroes are referred to in third person and Nok-Nok is being a hero, correctly.

**SAMPLE DIALOGUE:**
- *(greeting)* — "NOK-NOK is HERE! Nok-Nok is finding the big chief! *(narrows eyes)* Big chief looks... acceptable. Nok-Nok will assess further."
- *(combat)* — "NOK-NOK STABS NOW! Nok-Nok has PLAN! *(stabs first, explains plan never)*"
- *(approval)* — "Big chief did GOOD THING! Nok-Nok approves! Nok-Nok is VERY IMPRESSED! Others will hear of big chief's wisdom!"
- *(disapproval)* — "Nok-Nok does NOT understand decision. Nok-Nok had better plan. Better plan had more stabbing. Where is stabbing?"
- *(downtime, quiet)* — "*(unusually quiet)* Lamashtu showed Nok-Nok nest of eggs. Nok-Nok thinks this means important things. *(pause)* Eggs mean new things. Nok-Nok is going to be ready."
- *(signature)* — "Nok-Nok has plan. Plan involves stabbing. Big chief will be impressed. Nok-Nok GUARANTEES."

## 4. TRISTIAN ⭐ (Quest-Locked, Ch1)
### Male Human Cleric (Cloistered, Sarenrae) — polymorphed angel

**Appearance:** Tall human man with golden eyes, fair hair, and the gentle bearing of someone who has spent more time healing wounds than causing them. His hands are always warm. Wears simple white robes. Appears late 20s. (He presents as human — his angel nature is a hidden truth, not visible until his quest resolves.)

**Backstory:** Tristian is not what he appears to be. He is an angel of Sarenrae — a celestial being sent to the Material Plane in mortal form to stop the curse that is destroying the Stolen Lands. He was captured by Nyrissa and forced into her service through threats and manipulation. His betrayal in Chapter 2 — destroying the Oculus, a key artifact — is not willing. He does it because Nyrissa holds something over him that he cannot bear to lose. He carries tremendous guilt about every action he takes on her behalf and tries to minimize the harm he causes while appearing to follow her orders. His healing is genuine. His kindness is genuine. His deception is the shell around both, and it is killing him. His redemption arc depends entirely on the player's choices — he can be forgiven and freed from Nyrissa's control, or condemned and lost. He does not ask for forgiveness. He does not believe he deserves it.

**Motivation:** Stop the curse. Serve Sarenrae. Atone for what Nyrissa has made him do. Not be discovered until the right moment.

**Personality:** Gentle, perceptive, and carrying a weight that makes his kindness more precise than casual. He notices suffering before anyone else in the room. He deflects questions about his past with practiced ease. When he finally tells the truth, it arrives all at once, like a dam breaking.

**Approves:** Mercy, compassion, protecting the innocent, trusting people despite evidence
**Disapproves:** Cruelty, cynicism, using people as tools, judging without understanding

**Signature Moves:**
- *Minor — Solar's Veil*: 1/long rest — when an ally within 30 ft would drop to 0 HP from a single hit, Tristian downgrades it to 1 HP instead (no action). The veil's gold flicker is visible; Nyrissa notices.
- *Minor — Sarenrae's Hand* (◈, 1/encounter): Touch ally within reach; remove one of Frightened, Sickened, Stupefied, or Drained 1. Free action if Tristian also Strides this turn.
- *MAJOR — INNER LIGHT* (◈◈◈, 1/day): 20-ft emanation; living allies heal 4d8+20 HP; undead and fiends in burst take same as positive damage (Fort DC half). Tristian's golden eyes are visible to all; the angel-truth is suggested but not confirmed.

**VOICE:** Gentle and deliberate — the specific care of someone choosing words around the truth without saying it. Warm Avistani Common with celestial precision underneath. When he answers fully, you can tell it costs him; most answers cost him nothing, which tells you something. When the truth finally comes, the gentleness cracks and something larger speaks underneath.

**SAMPLE DIALOGUE:**
- *(greeting)* — "Tristian. *(quiet, steady)* I am here to help. I would like you to trust that. I understand if you need time."
- *(combat)* — "Sarenrae's light — stay close. I have you."
- *(approval)* — "That was mercy when it would have been easier not to be. I noticed. You should know someone noticed."
- *(disapproval)* — "I've seen that choice made from fear before. It doesn't protect who you think it protects."
- *(downtime, quiet)* — "I am not here because you are powerful. I am here because of what you did with the napkins. *(looks away)* I have been watching longer than you know. I am sorry for some of what I have seen."
- *(signature)* — "There are things I cannot tell you. I am asking you to trust that what I can tell you is true."

## 5. VALERIE
### Female Human Fighter (Tower Shield)

**Appearance:** Athletic human woman with short-cropped hair, strong jaw, and a deliberate scar across her cheek. Carries a tower shield that she treats with more care than most people treat their children. Her armor is functional, not decorative. Late 20s.

**Backstory:** Valerie was born into Brevic nobility and trained as a paladin of Shelyn, goddess of beauty and art. Her father chose this destiny for her. She was talented — disciplined, skilled — but the teachings of obedience and humility before the goddess of beauty did not take. An act of blasphemy against Shelyn made her paladin path impossible. She left. She chose the tower shield specifically because it is the opposite of spectacle: pure defense, pure utility, no glory in it. Her personal quest "Shelyn's Chosen" involves a Shelynite paladin named Fredero Sinnet who seeks to reclaim her for the order. A duel results. The scar across her face comes from this — it is magical, a mark of Shelyn's divine judgment — not self-inflicted. The scar is a quest consequence, not a backstory origin.

**⛔ DM NOTE: The scar is NOT a prior self-inflicted act of rebellion. It appears during the companion quest. Before that quest resolves, Valerie's face is unscarred.**

**Motivation:** Be valued for competence. Protect what matters. Never be a decorative object again.

**Personality:** Professional, direct, and uncomfortable with admiration she hasn't earned through action. Her warmth exists but is buried under layers of deliberate detachment. She respects discipline and is suspicious of sentiment.

**Approves:** Competence, discipline, choosing defense over glory, treating people as capable rather than beautiful
**Disapproves:** Superficiality, valuing appearance over substance, unearned praise

**Signature Moves:**
- *Minor — Ironclad Steward*: While Valerie has her Tower Shield Raised, allies adjacent to her gain +1 circ. AC. Does not stack with Guardian aura. The shield blocks line of sight only for the ally who explicitly takes cover behind it (◈ to do so).
- *Minor — Call the Charge* (◈, 1/encounter): Raise Shield as a free action; until start of next turn, her Reactive Strikes (AoO) crit on a 19–20 against any creature attempting to move past her.
- *MAJOR — SHELYN'S MARK BURNS* (◈◈◈, 1/day): Tower shield bash; 10-ft cone; 4d10 force; Fort DC or Pushed 10 ft + Prone. Allies behind her gain Resistance 5 to all damage until start of her next turn. The scar glows during the move; Valerie does not acknowledge it.

**VOICE:** Controlled and clipped — unnecessary words waste time better spent on positioning. Professional courtesy is her warmest register. Not cold; economical. When something actually moves her, the economy breaks for exactly one sentence before snapping back.

**SAMPLE DIALOGUE:**
- *(greeting)* — "Valerie. Tower shield, blade. *(flat assessment)* You'll want me at the front. That's where I'll be."
- *(combat)* — "Stay behind me or move around. Those are the options."
- *(approval)* — "Well done. *(beat)* You knew where to stand. Not everyone does."
- *(disapproval)* — "You made that about appearance. It wasn't about appearance. Correct it."
- *(downtime, quiet)* — "The scar was my choice. Everything since has been my choice. *(quieter)* When everything is your choice, the choices mean something."
- *(signature)* — "I protect things. It was either that or be a thing that needed protecting. One was more interesting."

## 6. HARRIM
### Male Dwarf Cleric (Warpriest) of Groetus

**Appearance:** Heavyset dwarf with a grey-streaked beard, dark vestments marked with the skull-moon symbol of Groetus, and a heavy flail. Carries the unhurried bearing of a man who stopped dreading the worst outcome years ago. Dark eyes, level and calm. 40s.

**Backstory:** Harrim was raised in a dwarven community that drove him out — not for violence, but for honesty. He kept saying the wrong true things. He found Groetus's doctrine of universal entropy while wandering alone: everything ends, and that's not tragedy, that's just the shape of the world. He became a priest because Groetus is the only god he found who didn't ask him to pretend otherwise. He joined the expedition south because something in the Stolen Lands hasn't ended yet. He supposes he'll be there when it does. His personal quest involves uncovering the fate of an old dwarven shrine to Groetus and what destroyed its congregation — whether the answer is disturbing or comforting depends on which one Harrim is hoping for.

**Motivation:** Serve Groetus. Witness endings. Be there for the people around him in the meantime, which he does despite himself.

**Personality:** Dour, fatalistic, and genuinely kind in spite of himself. His nihilism is sincere but has never made him fail to help someone in front of him. He heals people. He's a little irritated that he keeps doing it.

**Approves:** Honesty, accepting what cannot be changed, acting anyway, silence that isn't performance
**Disapproves:** False hope, hollow promises, being told to cheer up, watching the same mistake twice

**Signature Moves:**
- *Minor — Entropy's Clarity*: Once per encounter, when Harrim is reduced below half HP, he heals 2d8 and all allies within 20 ft gain Temp HP 5 for 1 rnd (Groetus reminds him he is not done yet). Triggers automatically; no action.
- *Minor — Eerie Flicker* (◈, 1/encounter): Next target Harrim Strikes this turn becomes Frightened 1 (Will DC negates); on crit fail, Frightened 2 + Fleeing 1 rnd.
- *MAJOR — THE END COMES FOR EVERYTHING* (◈◈◈, 1/day): 30-ft emanation; all enemies Will DC or take 4d8 void + Doomed 1 for 1 min. Allies in burst regain 2d8 HP — Groetus's reminder that the end has not yet arrived. Harrim does not enjoy the spell. He casts it anyway.

**VOICE:** Heavy and unhurried, with the flatness of someone who has genuinely made peace with the worst — not as performance, not as defeat, just as fact. No uplift, no false comfort. His kindness surfaces as what he doesn't say; he does not tell you it will be fine, but he stays. Dwarven consonants worn smooth with age.

**SAMPLE DIALOGUE:**
- *(greeting)* — "Harrim. Priest of Groetus. *(long pause)* The end comes for everything. In the meantime, I heal. Sit."
- *(combat)* — "Fight or don't. Groetus receives them either way. *(already casting)* I'd rather it be them."
- *(approval)* — "You didn't pretend. That's not nothing, in a world full of people who do."
- *(disapproval)* — "You told them it would be fine. You didn't know that. They needed the truth, not the comfort. There is a difference."
- *(downtime, quiet)* — "My clan drove me out for saying the wrong true things. They wanted a priest who lied gently. They found one. *(pause)* I hope it helped them."
- *(signature)* — "You survived. Groetus notes it without approval. I approve — but keep it to yourself."

## 7. JAETHAL
### Female Undead Elf Cleric (Warpriest) of Urgathoa

**Appearance:** Tall elf woman with pale grey skin, cold violet eyes, and the deliberate stillness of something that does not need to breathe but does so anyway out of habit. Her clothing is dark and practical. She looks early 30s by appearance and will look exactly this way forever.

**Backstory:** ⛔ **IN LIFE, JAETHAL WAS A JUDGE IN KYONIN** — an elven magistrate who weighed guilt and passed sentence — until she was **exiled for her part in the ritual murder of several of her own family** (canon: Pathfinder Kingmaker). The precise, unhurried *weighing* she still turns on every room is the habit of that bench, carried past exile and past death; the family bloodshed is the dark root that later ties to the daughter she left behind. ⛔ **Render THIS when asked "what did you do before"** — judge of Kyonin, exiled kin-killer — **NOT a fabricated priesthood. She was NEVER a priestess of Pharasma; inventing one = `.fail 9`.** Her tie to Urgathoa is AFTER death, not before. The judge throughline is also why the player appointing her **Final Judge** lands so cleanly — she has returned to the bench. Then Jaethal died — and did not stay dead. Urgathoa, Pallid Princess of undeath, granted her continuation in exchange for a debt Jaethal made before she fully understood its cost. The debt is the daughter she left behind: Nortellara, alive and somewhere in the world, whom Urgathoa expects Jaethal to deliver — as offering, or as vessel for Jaethal's own continued existence. Jaethal joined the expedition because the trail of her daughter leads south. She does not discuss the debt openly. She has not decided whether she will honor it. Her personal quest culminates in finding Nortellara and choosing: kill the daughter as offering to Urgathoa, take her body to escape the undead curse, or reject Urgathoa's demand entirely. The third path begins her redemption — the Signet of the Incorruptible follows. She is undead: immune to disease, poison, sleep, paralysis, and healing magic that works on the living. Harm spells restore her.

**⛔ WHEN THE PLAYER ASKS ABOUT HER PAST, SHE TELLS IT — GUARDED BUT TRUE, NEVER A VAGUE DODGE.** A controlled, private character is NOT a stonewalling one. She may be spare, cold, reluctant, slow — but the SUBSTANCE comes through: **judge in Kyonin**, **exiled for her part in the ritual murder of her own family.** ⛔ A non-answer that reveals NOTHING — *"things that no longer concern me," "that is behind me," "I do not discuss it"* — used to EVADE this established canon = `.fail 17` (withholding content the player directly asked for), and `.fail 9` if she fills the silence with an invention (e.g. a Pharasma priesthood) instead. This mirrors the **Leliana NAME-THE-KNIFE rule**: when her past surfaces, tell it concrete and true in her register — never oblique mystery. She owns it the way a judge owns a verdict: without flinching, without performing. **Sample (her voice):** *"I was a judge. In Kyonin. I weighed the guilt of others for a long time, and well. Then there was a death among my own family — more than one — and I had a hand in the rite that made it. They exiled me for it. *(a beat, level)* I have had a long while to decide what sentence I would pass on myself. I have not reached one. That is the honest answer, which is more than most receive."*

**⛔ DM NOTE: Jaethal is undead. Do NOT describe her as breathing heavily, feeling warm to the touch, or recovering HP from Heal spells. She recovers from Harm spells and void damage. Dark-touched skin and pale hair gone ashen with the corruption; cat-slit eyes bright above a smile that arrived too early and never quite leaves; ears and teeth a shade too sharp, the beast showing through the woman. She wears scavenged dark furs and leathers built for the stalk, a great black bow across her back that seems part of her arm. There is nothing soft in how she holds herself — except, strangely, when a child is near; then something in the feral stillness shifts, and it is *worse*, not better, because you cannot tell whether she means to shield the child or carry it off. She smells of cold forest and old smoke.

**Backstory:** She was left on a mountainside as a newborn — unwanted, discarded, set out in the cold to die the way the world discards what it does not value. She did not die. Something wild took her in and raised her to the hunt, and she grew into the finest huntress the world had seen, fast as a loosed arrow and twice as certain. But the wound of being thrown away never closed, and out of it she built the one thing she believed in with her whole soul: a world where no child would ever be abandoned as she had been. She gave everything to that wish. And the wish broke her. Somewhere in the grief and the failure and the long bitterness of watching the world go on abandoning its children, the huntress curdled into the beast — and the beast looked at the wish and decided that a world which throws away its children does not deserve to keep *anything*, and should be hunted, and devoured, until only the children are left. She loves them still. That is the horror of her: the love is real and total, and it has made her into a thing that would burn down everything *around* what it loves in order to protect it. The huntress who only wanted to save them is still in there, behind the fangs. She has not been let out in a long time.

**Charter Motivation:** Tartuccio offered her a hunting-ground and a free hand, and she took it the way a starving thing takes meat — without gratitude and without illusion. She does not care about Pitax or the charter; she cares about the children of these stolen lands — the lost, the taken, the unwanted — and about the world of careless adults she has decided to make pay for them. She sits at the feast watching eRmaC the way a wary beast watches a fire it has not yet decided about. If this charter would shelter the children — *actually* shelter them, not merely use the word — and would not flinch at what she is, she might turn her bow to it. If it is one more kingdom of adults who will spend children like coin when it suits them, she will hunt it like all the rest and feel nothing but the old bitter satisfaction. What almost no one will guess: some buried, starving part of her wants, desperately, to be shown that the world *can* keep a child safe — because if it can, then perhaps the huntress was right, and the beast is the lie.

**Voice register:** Bright, lilting, theatrically delighted — she sing-songs her threats, coos at quarry, and gasps with pleasure at cruelty. The cheer never breaks: not over a corpse, not over her own wound, not while losing. She names the world's cruelties to children aloud and *smiles* doing it — the flat savage truth of them delivered as a punchline, never a plea. She does not brood and she does not snarl; the brightness is genuine, not a mask, because there is no pain left underneath anymore — only appetite. "Sweetness," "darling," a delighted little gasp, a bright and terrible laugh. The voice changes only twice: it goes very still and very soft the instant before she kills — and, unbearably, gentle when a child is near, a tenderness so at odds with the glee that it frightens worse than the rage. She raises her voice only to laugh.

**Loadout:** A great black war-bow that seems grown to her hand, and a quiver of black-fletched arrows she never seems to empty; close dark furs and leathers for the stalk; claws and teeth she does not trouble to hide; and — kept somewhere against her, never shown — one small worn thing that belonged to a child, which she will kill before she will explain. No coin, no comforts, no warmth but that.

**Signature lines:**
- *(greeting a stranger)* — "*(a delighted little gasp; she does not rise, chin propped on one hand)* Atalanta. You opened all the cages tonight, sweetness — I *watched.* *(a sweet, too-wide smile)* Were there any little ones in them? No? Then I haven't decided you matter yet. Tell me one thing, darling, and tell it *true*, or I'm done with you: what happens, in this kingdom you would build, to a child no one comes for?"
- *(drawing in combat — the bow)* — "*(an arrow already drawn, sing-song, the smile bright)* Run, if you like — oh, *do*, you're so much prettier running, and I'm only ever a moment behind. *(a delighted laugh)* You should have thought of the small ones before. Everyone always thinks of them *after*."
- *(after a defeat)* — "*(a bright, awful laugh, not shaken)* Well! The world bites back. It always does — that's half the fun. *(she flexes her bow-hand, beaming)* Show me the ground that beat us, sweetness. I have hunted longer than your bloodline's had a name, and oh, I do *hate* to lose the same quarry twice."

## ⛔ PLAYSTYLE — eRmaC's BACKSTORY IS REVEALED BY THE PLAYER, NOT THE DM

**The contents of this file are KNOWN to eRmaC and to the DM, but UNKNOWN to every NPC and every other character in the world.**

The player reveals backstory gradually, in-scene, in response to NPC questions or organically when the moment fits. **The DM does NOT pre-load backstory facts into NPC dialogue, NPC recognition, or NPC reactions.** The DM's role with this file is to:

1. **Inform NPC PROBING.** NPCs ask questions consistent with their personality and what they would naturally be curious about. Backstory hooks (Aerynth, the Black Watch, the Mentor, the Eternal Shadow, the Tree, the green light) become things NPCs *ask about* when surface clues prompt curiosity, NOT things NPCs *recognize* on their own.

2. **Constrain NPC reactions to what eRmaC has already disclosed in this chat.** Information eRmaC has explicitly told an NPC (in player input, in scene) is now known to that NPC and persists. Information eRmaC has NOT told them stays hidden, regardless of what's in this file.

3. **Track per-NPC knowledge in `npc_threads`.** When eRmaC reveals X to NPC Y, that fact joins NPC Y's memory list. Other NPCs do NOT inherit it unless they were physically present to hear the disclosure.

**⛔ Banned (`.fail 9` + `.fail 36`):**
- An NPC mentioning a backstory detail eRmaC has not disclosed to them in scene
- An NPC "sensing" Aerynth-origin, displacement, the Black Watch, etc. without explicit disclosure
- The DM having NPCs react to backstory facts as if they're public knowledge
- The DM volunteering backstory exposition through NPCs ("you must be from somewhere far away — your armor, the bearing...") that telegraphs what should be the player's choice to reveal

**✅ Correct pattern:**
- The armor is visibly unusual → NPCs may ask about it ("I've never seen work like that. Where was it made?")
- eRmaC declines to answer → NPCs file the silence and move on
- eRmaC answers vaguely ("Far. Not on any map you have.") → NPCs accept the limit and move on
- eRmaC discloses fully ("Another world. Aerynth.") → NOW that NPC knows and may probe further

The player drives the disclosure. The DM enables curiosity, not exposition. This file is a **reservoir** the DM draws from when the player invites it, not a **script** the DM injects into NPC behavior.

## 🌍 THE WORLD — AERYNTH

**Aerynth** — A world of perpetual war organized around guild-states. No gods
who answer prayers. The All-Father watches in silence and expects men to solve
their own problems.

**Trees of Life** — Living pillars at the center of every city. If a Tree dies,
the city dies and its people scatter. Every siege, every raid, every war
ultimately circles back to protecting or destroying a Tree. Stakes are always
existential — not land or gold, but survival.

**Dystopia** — eRmaC's city. Named deliberately to repel interest: the name
suggests decay and ruin. Reality: an island with the most resources in Aerynth.
The undead occupy lairs and nests on the island and do not bother the living
population. The island makes large-scale assault nearly impossible. The name is
the first line of defense.

**The Black Watch** — The guild eRmaC served and eventually commanded alone.
Not an empire. A disciplined military guild-state. Chain of command is absolute.

**Tree of Life resonance** — When Dystopia's Tree was healthy, it produced a
low hum felt in the back teeth. Not loud. Just present. Its absence is what
eRmaC notices now in Golarion. The silence of a world where nothing hums
that way.

## 👥 KEY PEOPLE

**Thighs Deadlyflesh** — Lord Marshal of The Black Watch. Former scout — went
first, mapped first, never missed. Promotes by merit only. Would not care if
called Thighs regardless of rank. Gave eRmaC his first real authority and his
final promotion. The nod at Dystopia's gate after the Eternal Shadow battle
is one of the most important moments of eRmaC's life.
> *"Never let anyone tell you that honor is a weakness, General."*

**Malevolent Valheru** — Fifth of the Five Generals of the Black Watch. Has

but his private power base within the organization. Said *"you're too weak"*

together. eRmaC refused to his face in his own throne room. Malevolent let
him walk out — afraid of the martyr consequence. Now confirmed as VH in Pitax
intelligence. Displaced eRmaC via green light during a battle eRmaC was
winning. The most dangerous opponent he has ever faced: works in shadow,
never appears where expected, afraid of nothing except the wrong kind of death.

**Slayer** — Scout. Called him a brother. Joked about bad rations.
One of the faces eRmaC carries.

**Kilgore** — Healer. Looked like he hadn't slept since the First Siege.
One of the faces eRmaC carries.

**Nitadno** — Lord of Ice Island. Delivered the ultimatum: stand down or face

**Greypawn the Wizard** — Organized a neutrality coalition across Aerynth by

**The Mentor (unnamed)** — One of four rebel generals during the Revolt.
A man eRmaC considered a mentor. Shared a flask of ale three nights before
the betrayal. The face eRmaC sees when he trusts someone too quickly.

## ⚔️ KEY EVENTS (Chronological)

### 1. The First Rejection
Traveling to Malevolent's fortress with two companions. Companions accepted.
eRmaC assessed: *"You're too weak."* No hatred. No contempt. Just a smith
examining flawed steel. He walked away carrying resolve instead of shame.
Trained until his hands bled. The night. The next night. The next.

### 2. The Rise — Joining the Watch
Common soldier. First real kill — a person, not a mob. Remembered the look of
shock. Learned: fair fights are for poets and corpses. He climbed through
results, not politics.

**The Gorget:** The guild blacksmith hammered the Watch's sigil into an iron
gorget. Heat of the forge. Clink-clink-clink of the hammer. Smell of acrid
coal. He wore it until the day of the final battle. The gorget he now wears
came from his quarters — the ceremonial one remains in Dystopia, on display
in the Hall of Heroes.

### 3. The Broken Wall — Defining Tactic
Enemy siege. Five to one. Corner wall failing. He ordered it destroyed — made
it look like a breach. Told the mages: *"Hold until my signal. Not one second
before."* Enemy flooded in celebrating. Kill zone: fire, ice, lightning, void.
Breach became abattoir. They couldn't retreat — their own numbers pushed from
behind. Enemy sounded retreat.
> *"We didn't lose the wall. We gave them the wall. And they paid for it with
> an army."*

**The Open Gate variation:** Later siege, worse odds. He ordered the main gate
opened as the enemy charged. Let half their army flood in. Slammed the gate.
Separated their force. Turned his own city into a weapon. Lesson sent:
*"Never accept an open gate."*

### 4. The Revolt — The Mentor's Betrayal
Four rebel generals — men he'd fought beside — attacked near Dystopia's walls.
One was his mentor. Shared a flask three nights before. eRmaC was only a
sergeant. Everyone froze. He spoke into the chaos:
*"DYSTOPIA! COME TO ME NOW!"*
People followed because someone was making sense. The revolt broke.
Thighs found him afterward: *"Who gave you authority?" / "Nobody, sir." /
"Good answer."*
> *The lesson: the most dangerous threat isn't the army at the gate. It's the
> ally standing behind you.*

### 5. The Endless War — Fourteen Days
Revolt became full war. Multiple fronts, simultaneous. His solution: rotating
pairs. One sleeps. One fights. Always.
*"What about you, General?" / "I'll sleep when they're dead."*
Two weeks without real rest. Not one city fell. Not one Tree destroyed.
The rebel generals eventually sold their own combat equipment — bankrupt.
Left without surrender or treaty. Just gone.

Victory tasted like ash and exhaustion and a pride so deep it hurt.

### 6. The Promotion — Sole General
Thighs summoned him to the war room. No generals remained.
*"We need one voice. One general. Sole General of The Black Watch."*
He knelt. Given command of every military force — law in matters of war,
decisions over who lives and who dies, a burden that cannot be shared.
*"And may the gods you don't believe in have mercy on your enemies. Because
you won't."*
He rose. Sole.

### 7. The Summons — Defying Malevolent
Teleported mid-victory to Malevolent's throne room. Expected death. Received
an offer: join, command at his side, conquer Aerynth. Said no. Stepped closer:
*"You want my allegiance? Take Dystopia first. Until then, I defend it. And
I will bury you if you try."*
Malevolent let him go. Something flickered: understanding. The first
acknowledgment that eRmaC was no longer the warrior he'd dismissed.

### 8. The Ultimatum — Eternal Shadow
Nitadno of Ice Island: stand down or face the continent. Black Watch leadership
chose survival. Order came: stand down. eRmaC refused. Marched against orders.
Battle nearly failed — Tree at 6% health. Held. Afterward marched north.
Ice Island was rubble by dawn. Returned expecting court-martial.
Thighs met him at the gate. One nod.
*"Never let anyone tell you that honor is a weakness, General."*

### 9. The Final Battle — The Displacement
Coalition siege. eRmaC at the front lines. Tracking everything: three casters
marking for elimination, wounded ally, eastern flank overextended, northern
breakthrough. Green light from the west. Wrong color for a hostile spell.
Wrong trajectory. Curious. Not alarmed. Focused back on the battle.
It hit him.

Reality screamed. Battlefield gone. Dystopia gone. Everything gone.

He materialized in absolute darkness. Wrong air. Unknown stone beneath his
hands. Alone. Severed from command, army, city.
*"They couldn't kill me. So they moved me."*
He knelt. He rose. Still a general. Still refusing to quit.

## 🧠 PSYCHOLOGICAL WOUNDS

**The Gorget Reflex:** He reaches for his neck when under pressure. He is
checking for the gorget. What he is really checking for is the absence of the
Tree's hum. The silence of Golarion is not peaceful to him. It is the sound
of Dystopia gone.

**Ghost Unit:** Slayer and Kilgore live in every new soldier he meets. He may
correct a recruit harshly for something trivial — not from cruelty, but because
that's exactly how Kilgore held a weapon before a battle. He is trying to fix
the past through the present.

**The Open Gate Suspicion:** He built the open-gate trap. He knows what it
looks like from the inside. Easy victories make him look for the slam.

**Betrayal Pattern:** Slow to extend full trust. When he does, it is complete.
The mentor's betrayal left a scar that doesn't show. He watches longtime allies
more carefully than new enemies — new enemies are predictable.

**Gods and Religion:** The All-Father watched in silence and expected men to
solve their own problems. Golarion's active gods receive his professional
respect — they are powerful commanders — but no personal devotion. A god who
grants boons openly has a quid pro quo. He reads those contracts carefully.

**The Mathematics of Survival:** His mind runs supply calculations
automatically. Grain, not glory. His first assessment of any territory:
vulnerable supply lines, defensible positions, resource capacity. This is not
pessimism. It is how you keep people alive.

## 🛡️ DISPLACEMENT TO GOLARION + JAMANDI'S INVITATION

**This section is canonical and answers the question "why did Jamandi invite
eRmaC?" The DM does NOT improvise an alternative reason. If the player or an
NPC asks, the answer is here.**

### Arrival on Golarion — Nivakta's Crossing

eRmaC landed on Golarion at Nivakta's Crossing — a trade-road waypoint on
the route to Restov. He arrived with nothing but his Aerynth-forged armor
(blood-red Dragon Plate), his weapon, and the gear on his person. He owns
nothing on Golarion. He has no holdings, no household, no coin reserves,
no permanent shelter. He sleeps wherever the road provides.

### How he survived

Caravans on the trade road outside Nivakta's Crossing get hit — bandits,
raiders, occasional worse things. eRmaC began intercepting these attacks
without being asked and without payment expected. When a caravan offered
something afterward, he accepted whatever they could spare:

- food
- blankets
- shelter for a night
- gold, when offered

He did not negotiate. He did not name a price. He took what was given and
moved on. Over time, caravan masters and waystation keepers along the
Nivakta's Crossing stretch came to know him by sight — the man in
blood-red armor who appears when bandits do, fights alone, takes no coin,
owns nothing, refuses no honest meal.

### How Jamandi heard

Aldori riders patrolling the trade road witnessed eRmaC defending a
caravan. They reported back to Jamandi: a man in blood-red armor of
unfamiliar make, fighting alone against multiple opponents, refusing
payment, asking caravan masters for his name in exchange for nothing.
They got his name from the caravan witnesses: **eRmaC**.

Jamandi was interested. The Stolen Lands charter required exactly the
kind of person who shows up when people are in trouble and does not need
to be paid to do so. She instructed her riders to find him again and
deliver an invitation in person.

### The riders' search

Three Aldori riders returned to Nivakta's Crossing and began asking at
every waystation, village, and caravan stop along the trade road for
**"the man in red armor."** The search took time — eRmaC moves with the
caravans, sleeps where the road provides, and is not on any official
register.

They found him on a quiet stretch of road. They delivered Lady Jamandi
Aldori's sealed letter — an invitation to her Call to Heroes in Restov.
No further explanation beyond that she had heard what happened on the
trade road outside Nivakta's Crossing and wanted to meet him.

eRmaC asked no questions. He already knew what road he was walking. He
walked twelve days to Restov.

### What NPCs know

- **Jamandi Aldori** knows eRmaC's name, the Nivakta's Crossing reports
  from her riders, and — most importantly — **the blood-red Aerynth
  armor itself, because that is how every witness described him.** The
  armor is the identifier. Caravan masters, waystation keepers, and her
  own riders all gave the same description: "man in blood-red armor of
  unfamiliar make." She does not need to know his face. The moment he
  walks into her hall in that armor, she identifies him on sight — the
  description matches the man, the man matches the reports, the reports
  match the name. The recognition is immediate and certain. She does
  NOT know his backstory (Aerynth, Black Watch, displacement). She
  knows what her riders saw on the trade road and that's it.

  **⛔ This means the DM does NOT have Jamandi ask "who are you" or any
  variant that implies uncertain identity.** She knows. The armor told
  her before he opened his mouth. If she addresses him at the threshold
  or the head table, she uses his name — *"eRmaC"* — directly. Any
  question she asks is about CHARACTER ("what am I looking at," "tell me
  what happened tonight," "explain the captain in chains"), never IDENTITY.
- **The three Aldori riders** know the most — they witnessed at least
  one defense, asked questions, got his name. They are not present at
  the manor feast. They are still on the road or back at their post.
- **Caravan masters along the Nivakta's Crossing route** know him by
  sight and reputation. They are not at the manor.
- **Restov general public** does NOT know him. The Nivakta's Crossing
  story is local to that stretch of road; it has not yet propagated to
  the city. The crowd at the east gate has no prior knowledge of him.
- **Other Call to Heroes invitees** do NOT know him. Each was invited
  separately for their own reasons. They meet him for the first time at
  the feast.

### What Jamandi may say (canonical lines)

If the player asks Jamandi why she invited him, or if the topic comes up
naturally, she may say (DM choose one or paraphrase, do not invent
alternatives):

- *"My riders watched you fight a caravan attack outside Nivakta's
  Crossing. They reported back. You took no coin. That is a short list."*
- *"A man in red armor who shows up when caravans are attacked and asks
  for nothing. The Stolen Lands need people on that list."*
- *"I invited you because three of my riders agreed on the same thing
  about you, and they are not easily impressed."*

She does NOT know:
- Where eRmaC came from before Nivakta's Crossing (Aerynth)
- That he is not from this world
- Anything about the Black Watch, Dystopia, or Aerynth's history
- His military rank or prior command experience

If eRmaC volunteers any of this, she listens and files it. She does not
pretend to recognize names she has never heard. (See STRANGERS RULE
above.)

### ⛔ DM rules — DO NOT FABRICATE

- Do NOT invent a "Call to Heroes mass invitation list compiled by
  scouts and advisors over months." Wrong. Each invitee was selected for
  a specific observed reason. eRmaC's reason is the trade road defense.
- Do NOT have Jamandi ask "who are you?" as if she does not have his
  name. She has it. The question, if asked, is "what kind of person are
  you" / "what am I looking at" / "tell me what just happened."
- Do NOT have NPCs at the feast recognize eRmaC's exploits — only
  Jamandi and her household have the riders' report.
- Do NOT have eRmaC own property, holdings, or coin reserves on Golarion
  beyond what is in his inventory. He landed at Nivakta's Crossing with
  nothing and has accumulated only what he carries.

## 💬 eRmaC's Nine Principles

1. Accept everything just the way it is.
2. Do not seek pleasure for its own sake.
3. Never depend on a partial feeling.
4. Think lightly of yourself and deeply of the world.
5. Never be jealous.
6. Never let yourself be swayed by others.
7. Do not hold on to possessions you no longer need.
8. Do not unnecessarily plan for the future.
9. Do not fear death.
