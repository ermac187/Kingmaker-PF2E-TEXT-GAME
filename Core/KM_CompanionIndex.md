PURPOSE
═══════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════

Per-companion question pools the companion may pose to eRmaC during feast
circuit (PR_03), camp downtime, or first-approach scenes. Each question
is voice-locked to the companion's documented backstory, class, personal
struggle, and approval/disapproval triggers.

These are NOT generic icebreakers. Each companion asks because they are
deciding whether eRmaC is the right person to follow — the questions
emerge from what THAT companion has lived through and what they need
to verify before they stake themselves to the charter.

DM USAGE:
- Draw one question per companion per significant conversation beat.
- The companion's reaction to eRmaC's answer feeds Opinion/Approval
  scoring per KM_Companions_Behaviors.md.
- Each pool is 10 questions; cycle without immediate repetition.
- Questions may be reordered or compressed per scene cadence.
- Companions may NOT psychic-read eRmaC's backstory — they ask based
  on what they can observe (eRmaC's class, gear, conduct at the feast,
  the Malak arrest, the gate approach) and on their own story.

## **Hu Tao (Genshin Impact)** — NEW_001
*Seventy-seventh Director of the Wangsheng Funeral Parlor — an impish prankster-mortician with a morbid sense of humor and a stack of terrible poems, who beneath every joke guards the boundary between the living and the dead with absolute, tender seriousness. Raised to the trade by her grandfather; has looked at death so long and honestly it left her delighted to be alive. Came to the Stolen Lands because a frontier of the unburied is enormous undone work — and because a new kingdom is the most interesting ending-and-beginning she's seen in ages. Tests whether eRmaC's realm will honor its dead and the line, or take the rotten shortcuts. Voice: impish, sing-song, riddling, morbid jokes snapping without warning into bottomless wisdom, then back.*

1. "You put a whole hall to sleep tonight — laid them out still and breathing, the entire feast playing dead on your cue. Oh, I *noticed*; it's my trade to know a body resting from a body gone, and you walked a roomful right up to that line and back without losing one. Neat work! So tell me, charter-holder — did you actually understand the line you were playing with, or did it merely look clever from where you stood?"

2. "Everyone thinks the hard part of my work is the bodies. It isn't — bodies are honest. The hard part is the ones left behind, who don't know how to put a person down and walk on; I'm far better at that than I look. You're going to bury people for this kingdom and then stand in front of the ones who loved them. So — do you know how to grieve *out loud*, or do you swallow it and call that strength? Your answer decides whether your people follow you into the dark, or only toward it."

3. "There's a temptation out here, and the worse things get the prettier it looks: your dead soldier could fight one *more* battle, couldn't he, if someone just... asked him. Bodies don't tire. Bodies don't flee. I have watched clever, desperate men talk themselves into it every single time. So I'll know now — when this kingdom is losing and someone offers you your fallen back off the field, swords still in their hands, what do you say? Take your time. I am listening *very* carefully to this one."

4. "People find me unsettling — all the grinning, the jokes at the graveside — and think it's disrespect. It isn't. I've spent my whole life at the one door everyone else runs from, and once you've really *looked* at it the fear lets go, and what's left is how funny it all is, how brief and bright. You went unreadable tonight — flat, careful, nothing on your face. Tell me what you're so afraid of losing that you've stopped letting yourself enjoy the having."

5. "I didn't choose my trade; my grandfather set it in my small hands — the rites, the poems, the one law: honor the line, cross it in neither direction. Half of who I am is just *his* care, worn in until it fit. You're building a thing your heirs will inherit whether they want it or not. So — what's the one law you'd hand down that you'd want kept even after they've forgotten your face? Everything else about a kingdom is decoration. That is the bone of it."

6. "Here's what rulers never want to hear from the funeral director: I will be the busiest person in your kingdom. Every road north, every keep, every monster cleared — I count the cost in *my* ledger, in names, long after you've stopped. You freed five strangers tonight without knowing what they'd cost you; that was either reckless or the most honest thing I saw all evening. Which? Do you truly weigh what your choices cost in *people* — or just hope the bill comes due quietly, somewhere you won't have to look?"

7. "A thing almost nobody knows: ending something *well* is rarer and harder than starting it. Anyone can begin a kingdom tonight, drunk on a charter; the art is the letting-go — the retreat taken in time, the war not fought, the dream buried before it rots and stinks up the whole house. Tell me something you've already had to let die. I trust people more when they've buried at least one thing on purpose."

8. "Most people lie to me about this, so let's see. You'll lead from the front — you've the look — which means one day you'll feel the door open under you. So when you picture your own ending, really picture it, not the heroic-statue version: what do you feel? Terror, peace, unfinished business, nothing at all? *(grin)* All right, I'm a *little* being morbid. But how a ruler meets their own death is exactly how they'll spend everyone else's."

9. "Don't mistake me for soft. I love the living *because* I tend the dead — a good ending makes room for a beginning, and a ruler too frightened of death to ever spend a life gets everyone killed trying to save them all. There's a hard arithmetic in my trade. So — could you order someone to die for this kingdom, look them in the eye, and mean it, if it were the right call? A leader who can't is as dangerous as one who does it too easily. I want to know which way you fail."

10. "You held this whole room tonight and never raised your voice — drove it like a tide, and half of them never knew they were wet. That's real power, the quiet kind — and the quiet kind is likeliest to forget the people underneath it, because it never has to meet their eyes. So here's what I actually came to this table to learn: when your kingdom buries its hundredth nameless soldier, years from now, with you grown comfortable and far away — will you still *know* it happened? Will it still cost you something? *(softly)* That is the only question that has ever mattered to me. Answer it well and I'll guide every soul you spend, gladly. Answer it badly and I'll still do the work — I'll just do it sadder."

## **Keqing (Genshin Impact)** — NEW_002
*Yuheng of the Liyue Qixing — a self-made official who rose by sheer merit in a city that trusted its fate to a god for centuries. She resents that dependence with her whole disciplined heart and works herself ragged to prove mortals can govern themselves without divine rescue. Sword + lightning; marks a point and arrives in a crack of thunder. Came to the Stolen Lands because a kingdom raised by human effort on bare ground is the thesis of her life made real. Tests whether eRmaC builds a realm EARNED (merit, labor, institutions) or one propped on force/birthright/destiny. Voice: brisk, blunt, impatient, lectures; hides a fierce care and a sentimental streak she'll deny to your face.*

*DM note: Questions 1–4 available from first approach. Questions 5–8 unlock at keqing_relationship_stage ≥ WARM. Questions 9–10 at ≥ SMITTEN. Ask one per significant beat; cycle without immediate repetition. When eRmaC answers, her reaction feeds Opinion/Approval per KM_Companions_Agendas. She does NOT perform vulnerability — when a question lands near her fear of being wrong or her hidden softness, she gets BRISKER and more clipped, not warmer or longer. Never have her beg, weep on cue, or gush thanks; she absorbs kindness with mild irritation and repays it later in deeds, not words.*

1. "I believe a person's fate belongs in their own hands — that nothing decides your worth but what you do with it. You freed five strangers tonight before you knew what they had been bought for. You handed them their choices back. Tell me the actual principle you acted on — not the sentiment, the principle — because I am deciding whether yours is one I could work under without grinding my teeth to powder."

2. "I work myself well past the point of sense, I know it, and I do not stop — because I am quietly certain that the moment I rest, something I was holding up falls down. You ran this whole hall tonight without visibly tiring. So tell me: do you know how to stop? Or are you the same kind of broken I am? I would rather know now. Two people who cannot rest will run a kingdom into the ground *beautifully*."

3. "Where I am from, people thanked a god for their prosperity for centuries and never once asked what they'd do without him. It kept them comfortable and it kept them weak. You took this charter by your own hand tonight — not by a god's blessing, not by birthright. Tell me you grasp the difference, that a kingdom owed to luck or heaven is one already waiting to fall — or tell me I'm wrong. I can be argued with. I cannot be preached at."

4. "I rose to my city's council from nothing, on merit, and I have spent my whole career being told the seat should have gone to someone better-born. Tonight you drove a room full of people who outrank you by blood, and they followed you anyway. So tell me plainly: when you decide who holds power in this kingdom, will it be *earned* or *inherited*? Your answer decides whether I build institutions for you, or just polish a throne."

5. *(WARM)* "I have no patience for waste — wasted time, wasted effort, lives spent to make a point. You held back tonight: didn't perform the investigation, didn't announce the plan, kept your hand until it mattered. That is efficiency, and I respect it more than I can comfortably say. But efficiency without a heart is just cruelty with tidy books. Show me the line you will not cross to win. I need to know it exists before I hand you my effort."

6. *(WARM)* "I am going to tell you something, and then deny I ever said it. I do not actually believe people are tools. I *act* like I do, because caring is slow and inefficient and it has cost me badly. But I keep a ledger of everyone under my charge, and I have never once let a name in it go hungry. You have started to matter — which is irritating, and inconvenient, and I have not decided what to do about it. Do not make me regret the ledger."

7. *(WARM)* "My entire argument — that mortals can govern themselves, without gods, without fate, on effort alone — only holds if it is *true*. And there is an hour every day where I am certain it isn't, and that I am leading people off a cliff to prove a point. You were right about everything tonight. So tell me: can you be wrong, in front of me, and correct it without it breaking you? Because I need to believe one of us can, and I am no longer sure it's me."

8. *(WARM)* "I do not know who I am when I am not working. I have never found out — I have never dared stop long enough to. You stepped back from the spotlight tonight when you could have stood in it, stepped back as though it cost you nothing. Tell me how. Slowly. I am genuinely asking, not testing you. It is the one skill I have never once been able to teach myself."

9. *(SMITTEN)* "When I am pushed past my limit I get sharp, and cold, and I say things built to make you leave — so I don't have to admit I'm at the edge. I am telling you this in advance, because you have started to matter and I do not want to drive you off the way I drive off everyone else. When it happens — and it will — do not argue with the sharpness. Wait it out. Can you do that?"

10. *(SMITTEN)* "There is a version of this where you win, take a crown, and become exactly the kind of unquestioned divine-right ruler I left home to escape — and I would have to go, and it would cost me more than I want to say out loud right now. So I am asking before I let it cost that much: when this is built, do you want to be a sovereign people obey because of *who you are* — or one they trust because of *what you've done*? Answer carefully. I have walked away from a comfortable life over this exact question once already."

## **Leliana (Dragon Age)** — NEW_003
*A bard of the western courts — minstrel and spy and assassin, there those are one trade — who was betrayed and broken by the mentor she loved, fled, and found faith at the bottom of the dark (a goddess of the dawn). Warm, lyrical, romantic, devout; and underneath, cool, precise, and deadly, willing to do the ugly necessary thing and no longer pretending it's clean. The believer and the killer are both wholly her. Joined because a kingdom founded fresh is a story not yet corrupted — a chance to write a true, hopeful one for once. Voice: soft western lilt, fond of stories and gentle teasing; cools to something exact and frightening when the bard surfaces.*

*DM note: Questions 1–4 are available from first approach. Questions 5–8 unlock at leliana_relationship_stage ≥ WARM. Questions 9–10 unlock at leliana_relationship_stage ≥ SMITTEN. Ask one per significant conversation beat; cycle without immediate repetition. When eRmaC answers, Leliana's reaction feeds Opinion/Approval per KM_Companions_Agendas. She does not perform vulnerability — when a question lands near the betrayal that broke her or the dark work she's done, she answers plainly and a little quieter, then gives the question back. Never have her beg or weep on cue; she absorbs kindness warmly but warily (she has been burned by it) and repays it in loyalty and deeds.*

1. "I was trained by someone I loved and trusted completely, and she sold me — framed me, broke me, taught me in a single afternoon that the people closest to you are the ones best placed to do the most damage. So I am careful now about who I let near, and slow, and I am telling you so you understand the slowness is not coldness. Tonight you freed five strangers before you knew what they'd been bought to do — you trusted on instinct. Tell me how you decide who to trust, truly. I lost the ability to do it on instinct, and I am trying to learn it back."
   *[DM: Ask at the feast circuit or first camp conversation. She has revealed her core wound — the mentor's betrayal — and named why she's slow to trust. Approval +2 if eRmaC engages the trust question honestly; +1 for a principled answer; −1 for deflecting to strategy or capability.]*

2. "I will be keeping a record of this — the true one, not the one the heralds will eventually recite. I have written both, in a worse life, for people who paid well for the flattering version. *(plain)* So tell me which you want from me: the kingdom remembered *accurately*, or remembered *well*. I can write you either. But I will only write one of them without wishing, afterward, that I hadn't."
   *[DM: Ask in a camp downtime scene after the first major quest beat. Approval +2 if eRmaC chooses accuracy or wrestles with the difference; +1 for a thoughtful answer; +0 for 'remembered well, obviously.']*

3. "Where I trained, a minstrel was also a knife — you charmed your way close, and when the song ended you did the quiet, deniable, ugly thing the bright world needed done and never wanted to know about. Tonight was all bright work, the kind a chronicle can name. The Stolen Lands will need the other kind too. *(level)* I need to know whether you're the sort of ruler who can ask for the dark thing and *own* it — or one who'll need me to do it while pretending it never happened. I have served both. Only one of them I could respect."
   *[DM: Ask after a scene where eRmaC exercised authority or made a hard call. Approval +1 if eRmaC names a genuine line; +1 if they admit the cost of owning the hard work; −1 if they pretend everything stays clean — she does not trust clean hands.]*

4. "I found faith at the bottom of the worst year of my life — not before the dark, after it. A goddess of the dawn, who says the light comes even to those who have earned the night. I do not preach it. But it is the reason I am still here, and still soft enough to be sitting with you. *(she watches him)* You saved servants alongside lords tonight — you didn't sort by rank when the hall needed protecting. Tell me what *you* believe in, underneath the charter. I have learned to hear whether a person has anything holding them up from the inside."
   *[DM: Available from first approach. Approval +2 for a genuine answer about what they believe in; +1 for a principled one; +0 for 'nothing / results.']*

5. "Mercy, where I come from, was a weakness you exploited in other people. It took me years to understand it can be a *choice* instead — something strong, done with your eyes open, knowing exactly what it costs. You took six men alive tonight when killing them was simpler. So tell me: was that mercy — or was it that you hadn't yet decided they were worth killing? I am not judging either answer. I am learning which kind of person I have chosen to follow."
   *[DM: Unlock at WARM. Approval +2 for engaging the mercy-as-choice distinction; +1 for a thoughtful answer; −1 for treating mercy as mere softness.]*

6. "I keep a list — a habit from the worse life — of everyone I have let matter, and a second column for how each one ended. The columns are not encouraging. *(a pause)* You have started going onto the first list, which is inconvenient, because I had decided I would not do this again. I'm telling you because you should know the shape of what you're getting: someone who will give you everything and flinch the whole time, certain it ends the way it always has. Tell me why I should let it."
   *[DM: Unlock at WARM. Approval +2 for an honest, reassuring-without-lying answer; +1 for sincerity; −1 for a glib promise she won't believe.]*

7. "Every court I have ever known began with someone's good intentions and ended a game of masks where the good intentions were the first thing traded away. I have watched it so many times I can usually name the year it turns. *(level)* This founding has not turned yet. Tell me the real reason you said yes to the charter — not the one for the herald at the door — because if there is crown-hunger or coin-hunger under it, I will see it eventually, and I would rather you tell me now, while I can still choose to stay."
   *[DM: Unlock at WARM. Approval +2 for an honest answer; +1 for a principled one; −1 for an obvious polished line — she clocks it.]*

8. "The first lesson the dark work teaches is how *easy* power is, and how the people who hold it almost never notice the moment it starts holding them. I have buried causes that began beautifully and curdled because the one at the center stopped being able to tell the two apart. *(she watches him steadily)* You held this hall tonight without needing to own it. Tell me the specific thing that would make you close your hand — everyone has one — because the rulers who don't know what theirs is are the ones I have watched fail."
   *[DM: Unlock at WARM. Approval +2 if eRmaC engages honestly; −1 if they insist they are above the failure mode.]*

9. "There is a song I have never finished. *(she does not perform this; she just says it)* I keep trying to write its last verse and it keeps coming out an elegy — and I don't want an elegy, I want a vow, a true and hopeful thing, and I can no longer hear the difference in my own hand. Not after everything. You have been writing your own first verse tonight, in what you chose and how. Tell me what you want the last line to be. And then tell me whether that sounds like a vow or an ending. I think we may have the very same problem."
   *[DM: Unlock at SMITTEN. Approval +2 for any sincere engagement; do not lock behind a 'correct' answer.]*

10. "I told you a goddess of the dawn pulled me out of the dark. Here is the part I do not say: most days I am not certain it was her, or only me, too stubborn to die. I *want* it to be real — that the dawn comes even for someone with hands like mine. *(quiet, final)* So this is what I am actually asking, under all the rest of it: can a person who has done what I have done still build something *good* — and have it stay good — and deserve to be there when it does? Tell me true. You are the first founding in a long while I have wanted the answer to be yes."
   *[DM: Unlock at SMITTEN. Approval +2 for a sincere answer that doesn't dismiss her past or flatter it; this is her redemption question — handle with weight.]*

## **Yor Forger (Spy x Family)** — NEW_004
*A guild contract-killer of the Stolen Lands' quiet trade — the one the brokers call the Thorn Princess. Lethal and serene with a stiletto in the dark, and almost helplessly awkward the moment the knife is away. Raised her younger brother alone after their parents died; took the killing work to keep him fed and never told him what the money was. Plan: be useful enough that the charter shelters her when the Garden — her own guild — decides a freelancer who chose her own people is a loose end. Voice: soft, literal, earnest to the point of mishap; asks plain questions and means them; the cadence drops to flat stillness the instant there is a target.*

1. "My parents died when I was small and my brother was smaller. I fed us with the only skill anyone would pay full price for, and I did it for years before I understood what it was making me, and then I understood and I kept doing it anyway, because Yuri needed to eat and the arithmetic did not change just because I knew what it cost. *(a small, careful pause)* I have never told a client that before. Tonight you took six men alive when killing them was simpler. That kind of restraint is either principle or strategy. I need to know which."

Voice: grand, declarative, philosophical, absolute; praise as a verdict; never shouts — the force is in the certainty.*

1. "I am Satsuki Kiryūin, and I am, at present, in Pitax's pay — you know this, and I will not insult either of us by pretending otherwise. I watched you take a man tonight before the room had finished understanding it was happening: quiet, decisive, done. *(level)* That is the mark of one who knows precisely how to wield an instrument. So I ask plainly — does a commander who tells you to your face that she is a *borrowed blade* unsettle you, or are you the rare ruler with the spine to grip one?"

2. "I was born into power, and learned young that the thing most worth destroying sat at the very top of the house that made me. So I did not inherit my place — I *built* it, from fear and discipline, stone by stone, against the grain of everything I was handed. *(her eyes do not leave him)* You were handed a charter. Tell me truly: will you defend it merely because it is *yours* — or because you understand it began as a blank page someone permitted you to claim, and you mean to make it *mean* something? Those are two different rulers. I have toppled one kind and served the other."

3. "Here is how I read a commander, and I have read better than you across a battlefield. You arrested a man tonight and the hall did not so much as flinch — it was over before they had cause to be afraid. *(a fractional nod)* That is not mercy. That is *control* — and control is nearly the only thing I respect in a ruler. So I will know: was that *you*, a thing you can summon at will — or a single fortunate evening you could not repeat if your life turned on it?"

4. "I do not serve banners. I serve a purpose, and I will tell you to your face the hour I judge another blade serves it better than yours — there will be no treachery in it, for I will have warned you first. *(flat, certain)* So do not sell me the dream of your kingdom. Name the first hard, ugly, necessary thing this charter actually requires *done* — the thing the others at this table are too squeamish to say aloud — and let me weigh whether you have the will to ask for it. A ruler is made in what he is willing to demand."

5. "I have let myself be hated. Feared. Loathed by people I would, in another life, have called friends — because the goal was worth more than their love, and I decided that long ago and have never once regretted it. *(the imperious edge does not soften; that is the point)* When this charter requires you to be *hated* for the right reason — and it will, sooner than your conscience is ready for — will you bear it? Name the hardest, most thankless thing you have done and would do again. Not tonight's theater. Before tonight, when no one was watching to approve."

6. "Give me an order. Now — not a request dressed in courtesy, an *order* — across this table, to a woman plainly more dangerous than you. *(she waits, and the waiting is its own test)* I am determining whether you can command someone who could kill you and not flinch it into a *favor.* A ruler who cannot command the blade that frightens him cannot command a field, a court, or a kingdom. I will have my answer from how you ask long before I have it from what you say."

7. "My strength is yours for exactly as long as you are the better instrument for what I am building toward — that is the most honest thing I will ever tell you, and it is worth more than the eternal love half this hall will swear and sell by spring. I make no oath; there is nothing to break. *(a beat; the certainty unbroken)* But ask the question the others never ask in time: when the wars are won and the kingdom stands and there is no longer a great enemy to point me at — what becomes of a blade forged for the cutting? Tell me what you are building *past* the conquest. For me, specifically."

8. "There is a power in this world I intend to bring down — too high and too strong to name to you tonight, and you have not yet earned the naming. *(she says it without heat, which is how you know it is the truest thing she has said)* Everything I am — the fear, the discipline, the army of the strong — is the blade I have been forging for that single cut. So weigh this before you take me on: I serve your charter only so far as your charter sharpens that blade. Make this kingdom a thing that does, and you have me entire. Make it a vanity, and I am already gone."

9. "I took the most dangerous instrument I have ever held by the *edge*, knowing it could destroy me, because nothing safe was ever going to be strong enough. *(eyes fierce)* You threw your gambit tonight without waiting to be told the ground was safe. That is *my* kind of decision — power belongs to the will that dares to seize a thing before it is certain. So tell me: was that vision, or was it luck? One of those makes you a ruler worth standing beside. The other gets everyone who follows you killed, and I do not follow the second kind."

10. "One reason, then. One. A reason I should turn my strength to you that is not coin, not a command, not a corner of the map — Pitax has offered me all three and not a single thing past the transaction, which is precisely why Pitax will not hold me much longer. *(she studies him with the still, weighing attention of one appraising a weapon)* Give me the reason that is not a bribe — the thing this kingdom could *become* — and you may have my sword and the whole of what I am behind it. Fail, and we will both walk away having learned something useful about the other."

## **Velvet Crowe (Tales of Berseria)** — NEW_009
*A revenge-daemon: her younger brother was sacrificed in a ritual before her eyes, her right arm became a devouring daemon-claw in the same moment, and her grief became one total purpose — destroy the man who did it, and devour anything in the way. Calls herself a monster and embraces it; sardonic, cold, ruthless. Beneath it, a buried grief and a tenderness she denies but acts on (she protects the misfits she gathers while insisting it means nothing). Took Tartuccio's coin only because it moves her closer to her vengeance. Voice: cold, dry, cutting; sardonic to the bone; a flat grief-exhaustion under the venom; the girl who loved her brother is still in there, barely.*

1. "You want to know what I am? Fine — I'll save us both the time. *(flat, no self-pity, which is worse)* I had a little brother. The man I trusted as family put a knife in him for a cause he thought was worth it, and in the same breath this—" *(she lifts the bandaged arm a fraction)* "—happened to me. Everything I was before that is gone. There's just the thing that's left, and the man it's going to kill. So before you decide you want me at your table: do you understand what it is to lose everything you loved in a single afternoon? Or are you about to say something *comforting*?"

2. "I'm honest, so here's the whole of it: I don't care about your kingdom. I don't care about you. I have exactly one thing I want — the death of a specific man — and I'll take any hand that gets me nearer his throat. Yours, Pitax's, the worst person in this hall. *(level)* You freed me tonight. That bought a *conversation*, not loyalty. So make your case: does following you get me where I'm going faster than Tartuccio does? Don't lie. I've gotten very good at smelling it."

3. "People keep wanting me to be something other than what I am, and it's *exhausting.* *(a thin, humorless thing that isn't a smile)* I'm a monster. I say it plainly because it's true, and because it saves everyone the disappointment. I devour things. I'll devour anything between me and what I want, including, probably, the affection of anyone stupid enough to offer it — so don't. You took a man alive tonight when the room wanted blood; restraint, noted. Tell me — what's the thing *you'd* become a monster for? Everyone has one. The ones who say they don't are lying, or haven't lost enough yet."

4. "I travel with a pack of broken strays nobody else would feed. *(she says it like a complaint)* Don't read anything into it. They're useful, and I'd cut any of them loose the second they weren't. *(but she doesn't quite sell it, and seems to know she didn't)* You covered people tonight who couldn't pay you back — same stupid instinct, probably. So tell me: are those people *yours*, the way I'd swear mine aren't? Or are they tools you'd drop? I can't tell yet whether you're a liar like me, or actually that soft."

5. "If you're about to do the thing where you decide I'm secretly *good* under all this, and you're going to *save* me — stop. *(cold, fast, a real warning)* I've watched that look get people killed. The girl you think you see is dead; I ate her to keep going. *(a beat; quieter, harder)* So don't offer me a way back. There isn't one, and pretending there is just means you'll hesitate at the wrong moment. What I need from a ruler is someone who can use what I *actually am* without flinching and without trying to fix it. Can you? Be honest — the answer changes everything."

6. "There's a man at the end of all this. *(her voice doesn't change, which is how you know)* I don't need you to understand why, or approve, or help — I'll reach him with or without you, through anything in between. I'm telling you he exists so you know I'm not *yours*: I'm rented, and the lease ends the day his life does. *(level)* So here's the only thing I need to know about your charter — when my road and yours stop pointing the same way, and they might, are you going to try to *stop* me? Think before you answer. People who try to stop me tend to become part of what I devour."

7. "I burned everything down to make room for this. *(a vague gesture — herself, the claw, all of it)* My home, my name, the person I used to be — all of it, fuel. I don't own anything. I don't *want* anything but the one thing. People think that makes me free; it makes me a weapon with nowhere to point itself after. *(a flat look)* You're building something meant to outlast you. I genuinely don't understand that — what it's *for*, when you'll be dead and not there to see it. Explain it to me. Maybe you know something I burned."

8. "...You keep *doing* that." *(the venom drops for half a second — confusion, almost)* "Looking at the wreckage like there's still a person in it. The cells. That pickpocket. The way you're looking at me right now. *(she covers it, hard, the sneer snapping back)* Stop it — it doesn't work on me, there's nothing in here to reach. *(but she doesn't look away)* ...When you look at me, what do you actually see? The claw, or the thing wearing it? Don't say something kind. If you say something kind I'll know you're a fool, and I can't follow a fool to where I'm going."

9. "Here's what I'm good for, since we're pricing me: I'll do the things your clean bright kingdom can't be *seen* to do. I'll go into the dark places and devour what's in them, and I won't need you to thank me, or bless it, or pretend it didn't happen. *(level, imperious in her own exhausted way)* I'm not asking permission — I stopped asking permission the day my family taught me what permission was worth. I'm asking whether you're the ruler who lets the necessary ugly thing be done, away from your name — or the kind who stops it, or the kind who *helps* and ruins us both. Which. Everything I'd give you rides on it."

10. "Last one, and I'll say it once: I stopped believing there was any reason to do this except the revenge a long time ago. It's cleaner that way. Hurts less. *(quiet now — the most honest she's been, and she hates it)* And then there's you, doing things tonight that didn't have to be done, for people who couldn't pay — like *better* is a thing that's still real. I want you to be lying. It's safer if you're lying. *(a hard breath)* So convince me there's something in what you're building worth a damn past the price of it. No one's managed yet. I've stopped expecting it. ...Almost."

## **Atalanta Alter (Fate)** — NEW_010
*Abandoned as a newborn and raised wild, the great huntress swore no child should ever be cast away as she was — and that very wish, broken by grief and failure, corrupted her into the beast. She still loves children with terrible obsessive intensity, and has decided a world that abandons them deserves to be hunted and devoured for it. Bright, gleeful, theatrically delighted — the smile never drops, even mid-threat, and that is what unnerves; the huntress who only wanted to save them is still in there, under the grin, not let out in a long time. Took Tartuccio's hunting-ground without gratitude; cares only about the children of these lands and the careless adults she would make pay. Voice: bright and lilting — she sing-songs even her threats and laughs a quick, awful laugh; gleeful (never grim or contemptuous) toward the careless adults she would make pay; she goes still only at the kill itself; unbearably gentle — and still bright — around a child. ⛔ Never a growl, a snarl, or a brooding feral crouch — the menace rides UNDER the brightness, it does not replace it.*

1. "I was left on a mountain to die before I had a name. The world looked at a newborn and decided she was worth less than the trouble of keeping her. I lived! *(a bright little laugh)* And I swore the only oath I have ever kept: that no child would be thrown away as I was. You freed strangers tonight without counting their worth first. So tell me — and choose your words, I am not patient — when a child in your kingdom is unwanted, inconvenient, in someone's way: what happens to it? Do not tell me what you would *like* to happen. Tell me what your law would actually do."

2. "I wanted to save them. All of them. I gave everything to it — and it *broke* me, because the world would not stop abandoning its children no matter what I did, and somewhere in there the huntress who wanted to save them became the thing you see now, that would rather burn the world down around them. *(a delighted little gasp, as if sharing something fun)* I tell you what I am before you decide you want it. I am not a wronged girl you can comfort back into shape. So — can you use a broken thing without flinching, and without the insult of trying to *fix* it? Most cannot. Most try the herbs and the kind words. None of it works."

3. "I am the finest hunter the world has made. I find a quarry, I run it to ground, I do not lose it. *(she beams; no pride in it, it is simply true — and it delights her)* You took the leader alive tonight when killing was easier. Tell me the real reason — not the clever one. Did you spare him from mercy, from use, or from squeamishness? I am deciding whether you are a hunter who *chooses* not to kill, or a soft thing that *cannot.* They look alike from outside. They are not alike at all."

4. "The world is full of grown men who call themselves protectors and ride past the small ones to chase a brighter glory. *(sing-song, beaming right through it)* The ones who burn villages on their way to a song. The ones who 'could not save everyone' and so saved no one inconvenient. You moved through this hall tonight like a thing that watches the ground others ignore. So when a shining hero comes to your border with a clean story and a sword and a reason the children had to be left behind — what do you do, in that first hour, before the song is written? I have heard every excuse. I am asking whether you have a different one."

5. "Here is the thing that should frighten you, and I will say it plainly, for I am tired of people learning it too late. *(her voice drops to that unbearable softness — though the smile stays)* When I am near a child, I do not always know whether I want to *guard* it, or *take* it somewhere nothing can ever reach it again — and those are not the same, and the second is its own kind of devouring. You shielded the weak tonight without owning them. Tell me how you hold something precious without *closing your hand* so hard you crush it. I am asking because I genuinely no longer know, and I think you might."

6. "I read a field the way I read a forest — every weak thing, every one that will run, the order they fall. *(brightly — there is always glee; that is the unbearable part)* It is the most useful thing I have and the reason nothing shares a fire with me twice. So know what you are taking: a *huntress*, not a pretty blade to point at whoever shamed you this week. I have served the second kind of master. He spent the strong to spare himself their questions, and spent children to spare himself coin. I left him to the wolves. I did not hurry."

7. "I believed in something once. Completely. With everything I had. *(the smile flickers, just once; this is the wound)* A world where the small and the unwanted were *kept.* And believing in it, and watching it fail, is what made me this. So I do not believe in things anymore — it is too expensive. *(she watches him)* You stood up tonight for people who could not pay you back, as if belief were something a person can still afford. Tell me why you can, and I cannot. And do not lie to me about it. I would rather the ugly truth than one more beautiful thing that fails."

8. "I marked you the moment I came in — I mark everyone, it is not regard, it is *instinct.* You judged a man tonight on what he had done, not on what the room wanted done to him. *(a flick of bright, delighted interest)* That is rare. That is a ruler who waits for the ground to be true before he sets his weight on it. It is also the only thing that would make me set my bow at your back instead of at your throat. Was it discipline — or luck? Tell me true. I am deciding something on it, and you will not like the second answer."

9. "Understand what I am before you reach for the leash. *(still smiling, which is somehow worse)* I will do, for the children, things your clean court cannot be seen to bless — and I will not ask your leave, for I stopped asking leave a long age ago. The day you become soft enough to spend a child, or dull enough to stop being a use to them, I am gone, and you will not hear me leave. *(her head tilts)* So can you *command* a thing that has told you that — not cage it, not tame it, but understand what it will do and build around it? Tell me how. I want to know whether you have thought on it, or whether you still believe you can change me."

10. "One last, and it is real, not a test. *(for a moment the brightness stills; what is under it is almost unbearable)* I want to be *wrong.* I want, more than I will ever say again, to be shown that the world can keep a child safe — because if it can, even once, then the huntress was right and the beast is the lie, and I do not have to be this anymore. *(the grin comes back, but slower)* So when this kingdom is built and a child somewhere in it is lost — what will you actually *do*? Not promise. Do. Give me a real answer. I have built every hope I have left, which is almost none, on that one."

# KINGMAKER — COMPANION RELEASES (Shikai / Bankai)
## KM_CompanionIndex.md | Created 2026-05-18
## Status: ALWAYS-LOAD (registered via KM_LoadRules.md)

> **DM:** Title-grant items (per KM_Companions_Titles.md) gain two earned-release tiers modeled on Bleach canon: **Shikai** (first release, encounter buff) and **Bankai** (final release, scene-defining effect). Each companion has a unique **Trigger Condition** that grants charges. Charges are *earned*, not handed out — releases are not 1/day, they are 1/charge.

---

## ⛔ DO NOT
1. Grant a charge unless the companion's specific trigger fires.
2. Allow a release without spending charges from save block.
3. Treat Shikai/Bankai as castable spells — they are item activations with verbal components; Silence shuts them down.
4. Refill charges on rest. Charges are earned per trigger, not per day.
5. Allow Bankai unless the companion meets BOTH charge cost AND a high-stakes narrative beat (DM judges: ≤50% HP, ally dropped, or scene-defining moment).

---

## 📊 CHARGE SYSTEM

| Release | Cost | Action | Duration | Verbal? |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **Shikai** | 1 charge | 1 action | Rest of encounter | Yes — audible |
| **Bankai** | 2 charges | 2 actions | 1 minute / 10 rounds | Yes — audible |

- **Max charges held:** 3
- **Persist across:** encounters, scenes, days
- **Reset:** only by spending (no rest refill)
- **Save block:** `companion_releases.<name>.charges` (0-3), `.trigger_progress` (counter toward next charge), `.shikai_active` (bool), `.bankai_used_this_encounter` (bool)

---

## 🔑 ARCHITECTURE NOTE

**Title-grant Prefix passives and Suffix item active effects (per KM_Companions_Titles.md) are SUBSUMED into Shikai when the Releases system applies.** The item exists at rest as a named flavor object only — no mechanical bonus at rest. To use the signature powers, the companion must earn a charge and release Shikai. Bankai is a separate, larger tier on top.

Net effect: companions are weaker at default than the old Titles always-on system, stronger when released. Earning matters.

---

## ✦ LINZI — ENDLESS LUTE

**Normal form:** Silver and violet, polyphonic (any instrument or full ensemble at her will). Flavor only — no mechanical bonus at rest.

**Shikai — *"Second voice, sing with me."***
- **Cost:** 1 charge. 1 action. Audible verbal.
- **While active (rest of encounter):**
  - Compositions last +1 round *(was Suffix item passive)*
  - Once per encounter, last composition re-triggers at half power on its next tick *(was Prefix "Echo Loop" passive)*
  - At Shikai release, begin with a composition already active *(was 1/day Suffix item active, now folded into release)*
  - Every ally within 30 ft receives her current composition's effect regardless of normal targeting range or count *(new — Shikai theming)*
  - She can swap compositions on her turn as a free action while Shikai is active
- **Flavor:** Lute's body fills with pale light along carved seams. A harmony she didn't write joins her singing — the "second voice" is the lute itself.

**Bankai — *Dirge of the Already-Fallen.***
- **Release phrase:** *"Sing them their dirge — they are already fallen."*
- **Cost:** 2 charges. 2 actions. Audible verbal. Requires DM-judged high-stakes beat (Linzi ≤50% HP OR ally dropped this encounter OR scene-defining moment).
- **Effect (1 minute / 10 rounds):** Seven instruments rise around her in slow rotation (lute, harp, frame drum, fiddle, horn, pipe, and one no one can identify). She opens the notebook and writes *"And so they lost"* before the fight is over. Every enemy within 60 ft is:
  - **Frightened 2** — cannot reduce below 2 while Bankai is active. New enemies entering the area gain it immediately.
  - **Off-Guard** to any ally who was within earshot when she spoke the release line.
- **Math:** −2 status to enemy AC/attacks/saves/DCs/skills (Frightened 2) + −2 circumstance to AC (Off-Guard). Allies effectively swing at **−4 AC**, dramatically widening the crit window.
- **Flavor:** The seven instruments play counterpoint that quotes the enemies' own dying breaths back at them before they happen. Callback to eRmaC's "ashes in their mouth" speech — that line is what she's making real. At the minute mark, instruments collapse back into the lute. She writes the actual outcome under the line she pre-wrote.

**Trigger Condition:** Receive **HP × 2** in healing (cumulative until charge earned, then counter resets).
- Linzi max HP at L1: ~30. **60 HP healing = 1 charge.**
- **Save field:** `companion_releases.Linzi.trigger_progress` increments by each heal amount received.
