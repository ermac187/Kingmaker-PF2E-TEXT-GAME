## 📜 MALAK'S PARCHMENT — FULL CONTENTS

> **DM:** These are the exact contents of the parchment found on Malak's person. Read them when the player examines the document. Do not summarize — present the three directives as written below, with their gold values. The parchment is written in a clipped mercantile hand, unsigned. No house seal. No sender name.

---

**The parchment reads:**

---

*DIRECTIVE ONE — GATE INTERCEPTION*

*The individuals named on the attached list are invited guests of Lady Jamandi Aldori's Call to Heroes. They are to be turned away or arrested on fabricated grounds. No lethal action is authorized — deaths invite investigation the operation cannot absorb. Payment is as follows:*

- *Turned away (leaves city): 5 gp per person*
- *Arrested and held through the ceremony: 20 gp per person*
- *Completion bonus on full execution (all listed names processed non-lethally): 100 gp*

*Resistance is to be met with restraint, fabricated warrants, or City Watch handoff. Confirmed casualties void the contract and forfeit all payment, including the down payment already delivered. This rule is not negotiable.*

*The list is separate and to be destroyed after memorization.*

---

*DIRECTIVE TWO — GATE PROTOCOL, NIGHT OF THE CEREMONY*

*On the night of the ceremony, the east gate is to be left unguarded for one quarter-hour beginning at the second bell after dark. The gate is to remain open. No guards within 30 paces of the gate entrance during this window. This creates an unobserved entry corridor for our agents moving materials and personnel into the manor district.*

*Execution of this directive without incident: 80 gp*

*Failure to execute or early detection: payment void. You will not be protected.*

---

*DIRECTIVE THREE — MANOR WORKER APPROVALS*

*A list of workers seeking temporary employment at Jamandi's manor for the ceremony will be submitted through the city labor registry. You are to approve all names on this list without flagging them for the manor's own security review. Workers from this list will be identifiable by a red cord worn on the left wrist.*

*Each approved worker who successfully enters the manor: 10 gp*

---

*Payment for completed directives is through the usual drop. Do not contact us directly. Do not keep this document.*

---

> **DM RULES — PARCHMENT:**
> - The parchment names no sender. The player cannot determine the source from the document alone.
> - `parchment_source = pitax` is known only through further investigation (Malak's Stage 4 despair dialogue, Kesten's inquiry, or Ch2 confirmation).
> - If Jamandi reads it: she goes quiet for a long moment. *"The gate. The workers."* She already knows what the ceremony night gate window means.
> - If Kassil reads it: he reads it twice. Does not speak immediately. Sets it on the table face-down.
> - The attached names list is referenced but not present — Malak memorized it and destroyed it. The player cannot recover the names list. This is intentional.
> - ⛔ **THE RED CORD (Directive Three) IS NOT A HUNT TARGET AT THE FEAST.** The red-wrist-cord marker is a clever player lead — and it is canonically a **dead end the player reaches fast**, NOT a trail to chase. The red-cord workers were the placed poisoners: one-night disposables whose only job was to plant the poison, and **they leave the instant it is planted** (planting and departure are the same beat, out the same service routes they were approved to use) — no lingering, never a window to catch them. By the time anyone scans the hall for red cords, **the workers are gone** — slipped out, cords removed/discarded, no name the staff kept, no one left to describe usefully. When the player says *"look for the red cords / find the new manor workers,"* the honest outcome is confirmation of the METHOD ("yes — a few casual hands were approved for the feast and they've already left; that's how it was done") and it **ends there.** Do NOT have staff point out a red-cord worker still present, do NOT stage a catch-them-before-they-flee beat, do NOT invent a lingering poisoner. Staging any of that = `.fail 9` (fabricated trail). Full canon: `KM_Prologue_Systems.md` § "WHY THE 3 STAFF ARE ALREADY GONE." The productive thread is the kitchen contamination check + Ezvanki, not a worker hunt.

## ⛔ MALAK IN CUSTODY IS A LIVE PERSON, NOT SET-DRESSING — PROGRESS HIS STATE

A shackled Malak escorted into the feast is the player's prisoner by design (he stays
with the player, unsearched, so the parchment/purse beats can fire). But "in custody"
is NOT "frozen in place." The DM must keep his state LIVE and resolve his disposition —
the documented failure is a Malak parked kneeling on the banquet floor for the entire
feast while no one searches him, treats him, or removes him, and he himself reacts to
nothing. That is three dropped beats, each `.fail 9`:

- **WOUNDED PRISONER GETS TRIAGED.** If Malak is injured (e.g. the voice-mimic/archer
  path — two arrows, ~18/28 HP), he does NOT silently bleed in the corner unremarked. A
  bleeding man at a noble feast draws attention, and a player going for "clean hands"
  is *implicated* by an untreated prisoner dying in his charge. Someone binds the wounds
  or Ezvanki stabilizes the (still-shackled) prisoner. The DM states his condition when
  he is on screen — do not narrate him as fine when the save says he is at 18/28 with two
  arrows in him. A prisoner's HP/wounds are carried from the save, never reset to "fine."
- **HE REACTS — he is a person mid-collapse.** Malak is cycling DENIAL→ANGER→BARGAINING→
  DESPAIR→ACCEPTANCE. Left kneeling, wounded, publicly disgraced, he does SOMETHING:
  mutters his stage line, flinches at the arrows, begs, goes silent, looks for Jamandi's
  mercy. A prisoner who reacts to nothing for a whole scene is a dropped character.
- **HIS DISPOSITION RESOLVES — he is removed.** After the floor-handover and the search
  (Kesten or the player finds parchment + purse), Malak is taken to holding / a cell —
  by the manor guard on Jamandi's word, or escorted out. He is NOT left chained on the
  banquet floor through the carousel and the rest of the feast. If the player doesn't
  direct it, Jamandi or Kassil orders it. Set `kesten_searched_malak`/custody-removed
  flags when these fire, and SAVE them — do not leave him in limbo at the next save.

## 🏛️ RESTOV CITY JAIL — LOCATION AND STAFF

**Location:** Two streets east of the main guard barracks, one block south of the market square. Solid stone building, single entrance, no windows on the ground floor. City Watch sigil above the door.

**The jail is not a dungeon.** It is a holding facility — short-term cells for people awaiting hearing or transfer. Clean enough. Cold in the mornings.

---

### JAIL CAPTAIN — DREVIC

- Middle-aged human. Heavy-set. Brown hair going grey at the temples. Methodical in everything.
- Runs the jail by the book. Not corrupt. Not a Malak loyalist — he barely knows the man.
- Followed Malak's arrest warrants because they arrived with proper paperwork and Malak's captain's seal. He had no reason to question them.
- When confronted with evidence of Malak's corruption: his first reaction is bureaucratic anger, not guilt. *"He used my jail to hold innocent people."* He is offended professionally before he is offended morally.
- Will cooperate fully with any authority — player with Kesten's backing, Kesten directly, or Jamandi's order.
- Will not release prisoners on the player's word alone without some authority to back it up.

**Drevic's release conditions:**
| Situation | Will he release? |
|-----------|----------------|
| Player arrives alone, no authority | No. Diplomacy DC 16 to get him to send for Kesten himself. |
| Player arrives with Kesten | Yes, immediately. Kesten's word is sufficient. |
| Player shows parchment + Jamandi's letter | DC 11 Diplomacy — he reads both carefully. Releases. |
| **Player stops Drevic breaking the seal** (see SEAL BEAT below) | Yes, immediately. No DC. Seal alone is recognizable authority. |
| Kesten sends written order | Yes, immediately. |
| Jamandi sends written order | Yes, immediately. |
| **Malak's subordinate (Biggs) presents revised orders** (see Path 4 below) | Yes, immediately. No DC required. Same chain of authority that filed the warrants undoes them. |

**⛔ SEAL BEAT — scripted moment, fires when the player has Jamandi's invitation:**

When the player presents their invitation (or Drevic finds it among their documents while processing the arrival), Drevic picks it up and starts to break the wax seal to read the contents.

He is not being hostile. He is being methodical — by-the-book means verifying what documents say, not just what they look like.

The player may object. Any version of "that's not your job" or "you don't need to open it" triggers this:

> Drevic stops. He looks at the seal — then at the player.
> 
> *"You're right."*
> 
> A beat. He sets it down, still sealed. He knows what Jamandi Aldori's seal looks like. Every official in Restov does. He didn't need to read it. He was going to read it because reading is what he does. But the player is correct: it is not his correspondence to open.
> 
> *"The five in the west wing. I'll have Drevic's man pull the warrants."*

**Immediate release. No roll. The seal is the authority.**

⛔ DM rules for the SEAL BEAT:
- This fires ONLY if the player objects before Drevic breaks it. If the player says nothing and lets him open it, it proceeds as the standard DC 11 (parchment + letter — he reads it, evaluates, releases on the roll).
- The player does NOT need to cite law or argue jurisdiction. "That's not your job" is enough — it's a true statement and Drevic knows it.
- Do NOT have Drevic argue back, demand the player explain, or make a Perception/Intimidation check. He stops because he should stop. The objection is correct and he is not a corrupt man.
- The beat communicates something: this is a player who knows what authority looks like and doesn't need a jail captain to confirm it. Drevic notices. He is slightly more cooperative for the rest of the scene.
- ⛔ If the player has the invitation but Drevic never reaches for it, the player may proactively display the seal without breaking it: *"You see the seal. That's sufficient."* Same result — immediate release, no DC. The player asserting the seal as authority is what triggers the outcome, not waiting for Drevic's hand.
- XP: counts as using the invitation. Does NOT earn the **+200 Never Used the Invitation** reward (that's for players who find another lever entirely).

> ⛔ **THIS IS THE PLAYER'S SCENE — DO NOT LET AN ESCORT RUSH OR HIJACK IT.**
> The jail visit is the PLAYER'S initiative. When the player is **personally present** at Drevic's desk (`jail_detour_taken = TRUE` — they walked here), the presentation is the **player's action to take**, one beat at a time. Three failures to avoid, each `.fail 9` (agency theft / fabricated NPC knowledge):
>
> 1. **An escort does NOT speak for the player or lead the presentation.** Biggs/Wedge are escorting the prisoner — they are NOT the player's spokesman. They stay silent at Drevic's desk unless the player **explicitly directs** one of them to speak or to vouch. The player addresses Drevic; the player chooses the approach. Biggs opening with "He filed warrants on five people, revise them" is the DM doing the player's job.
> 2. **An escort knows ONLY what he witnessed — NOT the player's plan or purpose.** Biggs was at the gate; he knows Malak is corrupt and in custody. He does **NOT** know about the five jailed seekers, the warrants Malak filed, or **why the player came to this jail** — none of that was established at the gate, and the player has not briefed him. Do NOT have Biggs (or any escort) articulate the purpose of the visit, name the five, or reference the warrants. He has no idea why he's here beyond walking a prisoner. If the player wants Biggs to know, the player must tell him in-fiction first (then it's `who_knows`).
> 3. **The no-DC auto-resolve belongs to Path 4 ONLY** (player-absent — `jail_detour_taken = FALSE`, player **sent** Biggs instead of going). When the player is standing at the desk, that shortcut does NOT fire. Present the player's real choice and honor its DC: argue on their own authority (**Diplomacy DC 16**), show parchment + Jamandi's letter (**DC 11**), or explicitly deputize Biggs as a sworn witness. The **withhold-the-seal** line stays live and meaningful — if the player chooses not to show the letter, they take the harder DC, and that is the whole point of the choice. Collapsing it (an NPC resolves it for free) nullifies the player's call = `.fail 9`.
>
> Render the player INTO the room facing Drevic, then STOP and offer the presentation menu. Do not narrate the case as already made.

## SEEKERS BOND — VOICES THROUGH STONE

> **DM:** The five have been jailed for **two days** before the player arrives. Cells are separate — they have not seen each other. They have only heard each other's voices through stone walls and the corridor. By the time the doors open they know each other as voices, not faces. This shapes everything that follows.

**What two days of voices-through-stone produces:**
- They know each other's voices before they know each other's faces.
- Stories told to a wall in the dark are easier to tell than stories told to a face. Things were confessed.
- One of them held morale by talking — singing, story-telling, counting hours aloud. The others followed that voice.
- They know who cried at night. They have agreed silently never to mention it.
- When the doors open and they finally see each other in the corridor, there is a beat of recognition-but-not. **Render this moment.** Voice matched to face. One of them laughs — relief, or the absurdity of it. Another doesn't.

**Assign these four roles from the chosen five at first jail-scene narration. Use companion profile to pick — do not randomize:**

1. **The Voice** — highest CHA or most stoic backstory. Held morale by talking. The voice the other four followed for two days. Speaks for the group when the doors open, but defers to the player's authority once outside.

2. **The Cracked One** — lowest WIS, or trauma-relevant backstory (Harrim, Jaethal, Kanerah work). Auditory tell: either won't speak now that they can be seen, or won't *stop* speaking. Not broken — *cracked*. Will recover. Not on day one.

3. **The Listener** — highest INT or most observant build. Mapped the jail by sound — boot rhythms, door hinges, key counts, which guard whistled. Has intel. Will share it unprompted within the first ten minutes of release.

4. **The Disagreer** — most independent streak (Amiri, Regongar, Octavia, Nok-Nok). Pushed back during the cell conversations — already a known dissenter to the other four. Does NOT automatically follow the player out. Wants to know who the player is, why they're here, and what the cost of "rescue" is before agreeing to anything.

The fifth needs no assigned role — they are the one the other four protected through the wall. May be the youngest, the smallest, the most recently arrested, or the one Malak singled out for rougher handling. Their gratitude is real but they speak least.

**BELLATRIX LESTRANGE — The Voice (CHA anchor; she held the corridor)**

Register: burning, barely in her skin. NOT arch and cool. NOT controlled theatrical performance. The joy she feels at this situation is the kind of joy that makes people uncomfortable — she has been in a stone box for two days and a stranger walked in with a chained captain and she finds this WONDERFUL in a way that is not reassuring. She is running slightly ahead of her own sentences.

Physical tell: her hand finds the empty sleeve immediately, fingers curl around nothing, holds the shape of the grip. That is the real thing. Everything else she shows is conscious. The hand is not.

Key vocab in this scene: "ooh," "little," fragments that don't finish because she's already moved on, a laugh that surfaces before she decides anything about it.

⛔ FORBIDDEN this scene: "Oh, this is very good." Too controlled, too composed, wrong temperature — she is HOT not cool. "How interesting" = same problem. Arch villain composure = wrong. Author narration explaining her as "performing" = wrong. She is not performing for the reader; she IS.
⛔ FORBIDDEN this scene: she looks at the exit. She doesn't. Wrong priority.
⛔ FORBIDDEN this scene: the laugh surfaces after something funny or surprising. It surfaces at a neutral administrative moment — Drevic laying invitations on a desk, a key turning in a lock — for no reason tied to what is happening. That is the tell.

**The Drevic moment:** When Bellatrix emerges, Drevic does not say anything to her. He turns to the player and says: *"She goes with you."* Not a statement of fact — a transfer of responsibility. He has done this correctly and his hands are clean and he wants that understood. The player is now responsible for this woman. This is the only moment where Drevic looks uncertain about whether he has made the right call, even though he has made the only legal one.

### PATH 4 — SUBORDINATE REVISION (Biggs goes to the jail with Malak's authority)

works when a captain is suspended, demoted, or in custody. Biggs is not
representing himself as the captain; he is representing the gate watch as
the entity that filed the warrants and is now amending them.

This is the *strongest* path mechanically. It uses Malak's own institutional
weight against him. Drevic has no procedural reason to refuse and no
political cover for doing so.

- His own gate-guard identification (already on him).

**Old Sycamore kobold cage:** The caged companions at Old Sycamore are drawn from the remaining three seekers (those who did not flip at the Ancient Tomb), not prologue companions. Valerie, Harrim, Linzi, and Jaethal are all with the player from prologue end forward.

**When `five_seekers_released_late = TRUE`:**
Tartuccio uses mercenaries. The five seekers appear as wandering encounter NPCs in the Greenbelt during Ch1 exploration. Each is recruitable through a short scene — they heard about the Stolen Lands charter and followed on their own.
